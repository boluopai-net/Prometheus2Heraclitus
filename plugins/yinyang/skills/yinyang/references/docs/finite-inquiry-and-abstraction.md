# Finite inquiry and economical abstraction

[Home](../../README.md) · [简体中文](finite-inquiry-and-abstraction.zh-CN.md)

The shared trajectory has now acquired several connected forms: versions and
operation traces, expressions of experience, questions and alternatives,
choices and their grounds. How much of this must be represented and brought
back into attention for the participants to continue or reflect? Limited
time, partial access, conflicting material, and costly representations make
that choice consequential. A history too thin to reconnect a conclusion with
its sources loses its usefulness; an indiscriminate accumulation can make the
relevant passage impossible to find.

In the reading environment, the current interface and a summary of the latest
decision may suffice to resume work. Asking why the layout changed calls for
the earlier version, the interruption reported during reading, the alternatives
considered, and the feedback on the revision. These questions need different
amounts of the same trajectory. Economy concerns what must be recoverable
for a warranted answer, rather than how small an account can be made.

A concrete question is: how can one get from the house
to the windmill? The detailed town and its simple map share the same endpoints,
river, bridge, and connected road. Roof tiles, flowers, and individual stones
can be omitted for this question; the crossing cannot. If the bridge closes,
that change must reach the map or its route will mislead. Economy comes from
preserving what the answer depends on, with less to represent and inspect.

The [preference harness](preference-harness.md) distinguishes choosing working
views from determining what they support. That distinction makes economy
assessable: which relations and answers must an abstraction preserve, and what
can a feasible method obtain under the stated support conditions? This is a
connection between questions, not a fixed chronology. In actual inquiry,
abstraction, view selection, and inference can interleave and prompt one
another's revision.

This orientation has two illuminating comparisons: philosopher Professor Feng Ye's [*Studies in No-Self Physicalism*, §§1.1 and 6.5](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-981-19-8143-2) separates ideal reduction from actual methods of inquiry; a local formal resource distinguishes what can safely be inferred from what can feasibly be computed. Their different commitments bring a common question into focus: how a finite inquirer obtains definite knowledge through a selective representation and a bounded method.

## A retrievable trajectory under finite attention

A useful account can give the present purpose, current understanding, result,
and unresolved question a compact expression, while links lead back to the
versions, operations, or feedback that support it. Retrieving one relevant
episode need not load every episode into the active context. What is left
outside attention can remain available at its source; what was never recorded
remains a gap rather than a detail that a fluent summary may invent.

For the reading case, “direct passage access replaced the shelf” is too thin
if reflection concerns why the first design seemed sufficient. Retaining that
it passed an opening test, yet interrupted actual reading, preserves a
consequential relation between an operational check and the person's purpose.
Individual rendering details may be irrelevant to that question. If a later
problem concerns a broken layout, some of those details become relevant again.
Sufficiency follows the inquiry and can change with it.

The map comparison above specifies the same discipline: retain the crossing
on which the route depends. The physical and formal comparisons below develop
its limits. A manageable description does not prove that reality contains
fewer kinds of things; a method preserving specified formal answers does not
by itself prove that an account of an actual episode is faithful. Both
distinctions keep selective retention answerable to what it is meant to explain.

## Computational articulation and physical reduction

[Naturalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/naturalism/) alone does not imply
physicalism: locating inquiry and its objects within nature does not settle
every claim about their physical basis. [Physicalism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/physicalism/),
in turn, need not entail a complete conceptual or theoretical reduction, or a
feasible calculation. Lacking a demonstrated reduction today does not by itself
refute physicalism. These distinctions let a commitment to physical reality
coexist with open questions about explanation and computation.

The framework adopts physical reality as its common basis and computational
ontology as an upper-level abstraction of it. Merely naming that basis does
not yet select the variables, representations, and operations that make a
particular inquiry calculable. Even an in-principle reduction would not by
itself provide a feasible algorithm for the present question. Abstraction
preserves the relations that matter while setting aside detail whose cost
does not help answer it; its adequacy remains open to evidence and use.

A computational ontology makes relations available for inquiry. A functional description explains an activity through what its parts do and how their transformations connect. It can guide investigation while the microphysical realization is still being studied. Physical reduction asks a further question: how those descriptions connect to a proposed physical basis, through which definitions, identifications, or bridge assumptions. The success of a functional account and the adequacy of such a reduction therefore call for different evidence.

Several questions often hidden inside “reducible” need separate answers:

| Question | What an affirmative answer would establish |
| --- | --- |
| Physical dependence or realization | The phenomenon occurs through physical conditions or mechanisms. |
| Ontological reduction | The entities invoked require no additional independently existing kinds beyond the proposed basis. |
| Analytic reduction | Higher-level concepts can be reduced through meanings or definitions. |
| Scientific identification | An appropriate physical account identifies the higher-level phenomenon's instances. |
| Derivability | Higher-level truths or laws follow from a specified theory, bridge assumptions, and description. |
| In-principle possibility | A stated idealized description and reasoning capacity suffice, even if unavailable in practice. |
| Feasible computation | An actual method can obtain the result within the relevant resource limits. |

Each question specifies a different relation. Derivability from an ideal complete physical description differs from derivability from current physics. Establishing dependence locates a phenomenon's material conditions; deriving its higher-level relations additionally needs an account of how those conditions realize the distinctions in question. A missing present derivation leaves that bridge to be investigated. Meanwhile, psychological, social, and computational inquiry can develop the relations needed to understand and act.

## A public comparison with Professor Feng Ye

Professor Feng Ye's *Studies in No-Self Physicalism* develops an explicitly physical account of cognitive subjects and connects it with representation, truth, mathematics, epistemology, and ontology. Its scope includes methodology, not metaphysics alone. [The publisher's record](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-981-19-8143-2) identifies the book and its chapters; the detailed comparison has textual locators in §1.1, p. 6; §6.5, pp. 445–447 and 456–459; and §7.4.2, pp. 528–532.

Those passages distinguish practical limitations from ideal reduction and allow non-reductive methods where complexity prevents a complete neurological explanation. Mental predicates need not analytically contain physical predicates. The account distinguishes ideal descriptions and reasoning powers from actual ones; derivability from present physics remains a separate question. Methodological naturalism permits suitably reliable intuitions while examining their conditions and limits. Pattern recognition does not by itself justify a stronger explanatory claim.

The substantive meeting point is the difference between what an ideal account might explain and how a finite inquirer actually obtains understanding. Professor Feng Ye combines a physicalist commitment with attention to complexity, representation, intuition, and reliability. YinYang shares those methodological concerns while developing a question-relative computational account whose use rests on the relations it explains and the actions it enables. Its local functional argument leaves ultimate reduction open. The framework's physical grounding does not settle every claim about reduction; a factual claim about its possibility or impossibility still needs an argument connecting the proposed basis with the phenomenon. This separates a chosen worldview, a useful method, and a conclusion about realization.

The [2021 mathematics lecture report](https://renwen.ucas.ac.cn/index.php/rczp/2015-12-09-08-37-01/55005-2021-6), from the hosting institution, distinguishes fictional mathematical objects from true applied descriptions of physical reality. It supplies another comparison: constructed objects can contribute to a true application through their representational role. Their usefulness and the truth of an applied description concern different relations. This helps articulate how a construction can matter to inquiry while its ontological commitment is assessed separately.

Further public sources are Professor Feng Ye's [“为什么相信还原的物理主义” (2017), *Academic Monthly* 49(2), 34–43](https://www.xsyk021.com/article/id/5f3fe6a3-6f09-4779-9b31-33c580cb619f), which distinguishes ontological and epistemological reduction and in-principle versus practical reduction, and his [2024 interview on logic](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_28071984), which discusses non-linguistic cognition and explanatory models. Together these sources locate the comparison in his stated philosophical and methodological positions.

## What a local formal resource makes precise

Public [research on prioritized knowledge bases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05746v5) studies selected questions of inference under inconsistent data. It fixes a formal setting, separates ontology from assertions, distinguishes repair selection from query acceptance, and examines computational cost. Reading these distinctions as resources for finite inquiry is this essay's philosophical interpretation. The fixed setting makes the consequences of particular assumptions checkable, supplying a precise local comparison for the wider activity of developing and revising views.

To investigate computational cost, the formal problem holds specified choices
stable while obtaining answers. An inquiry can later revise those choices,
but its cost comparison needs a defined target. This supplies the bridge to
approximation: obtain a restricted set of answers whose relation to that target
can be proved, then ask whether the restriction is useful for the actual task.

## Three forms of restrained commitment

**Ontological restraint** keeps the scope of a commitment matched to the relation established. Material conditions can be studied at the level needed by the question, and functional relations can be used while their ultimate reduction remains open. An orientation gives inquiry its starting direction; evidence and argument determine which factual conclusions follow.

**Skeptical acceptance under conflict** limits accepted conclusions to those
meeting a stated support policy. When that policy requires every admissible
candidate to entail $q$, disagreement can block acceptance. Non-acceptance of
$q$ is not acceptance of $\neg q$. This restraint concerns how much the evidence
warrants; it can require substantial computation rather than save it.

**Computational restraint** uses a sound lower approximation that may omit answers while preserving a specified formal target's guarantees. This can reduce cost, but savings depend on what the construction and checks actually require. Adequacy asks whether the answers suffice for the present question. An answer's formal soundness and its adequacy as a description of the world are assessed at their respective boundaries.

## A bounded approximation example

In Bienvenu and Bourgaux's [repair/argumentation construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05746v5),
a knowledge base separates general axioms $T$ from a finite assertion set $A$.
A repair is an inclusion-maximal subset of $A$ consistent with $T$; priorities
select preferred repairs. Assume consistent $T$, individually $T$-consistent
assertions, and the paper's specified conflict-based argumentation construction.
Let $\mathcal R_P$ be its nonempty family of Pareto-optimal repairs.

The corresponding argumentation framework organizes conflict through attack
and defense relations. Its grounded extension $G$ identifies assertions accepted
under grounded semantics and is
contained in every stable extension. Under the stated correspondence between
stable extensions and Pareto-optimal repairs, this gives:

$$
G\subseteq\bigcap_{R\in\mathcal R_P}R,\qquad
\langle T,G\rangle\models q
\Longrightarrow
\left\langle T,\bigcap_{R\in\mathcal R_P}R\right\rangle\models q.
$$

The consequence implication uses monotonicity of classical entailment:
adding premises preserves consequences. P-IAR reasons from the assertions
common to all Pareto-optimal repairs. Thus reasoning from $G$ supplies a sound
lower approximation to P-IAR under these assumptions: every answer obtained
this way is a P-IAR answer, but some P-IAR answers may be missed. This compares
a candidate method with a specified target, rather than changing
what the target means. P-AR has a different target—entailment in every selected
repair—and may obtain common consequences through different assertions.
[Selection and inference](preference-harness.md#selection-and-inference) develops
that distinction.

This conditional derivation establishes inclusion. Its computational benefit must be assessed through the cost of conflict construction, consistency and query checks, and the components held fixed. Those operations determine the runtime; the inclusion itself specifies which answers the approximation preserves.

## Abstraction, premises, and explanatory gain

Economical abstraction creates enough concepts and relations for the current question, then unfolds deeper structure when it matters. This concerns active representation and inference burden, which differs from the number of ultimate entity kinds. A sparse metaphysics can still require expensive computation.

Indirectly inferred objects and useful hypotheses extend inquiry beyond present observation. Their support comes from the explanatory and predictive relations connecting them with accessible evidence. Several workable ontologies can make different relations visible; selecting among them concerns the question, the relevant evidence, and the cost of using each view. Evidence remains able to resist the selected description.

Searching backward for sufficient premises clarifies what a conclusion requires. A strong premise validly implying a weaker conclusion has explanatory value insofar as its independent support and cost are warranted. Putting the desired answer into an assumption can conceal the difficulty. The useful gain is identifying premises that can themselves be investigated. This everyday backward search can be distinguished from reverse mathematics, a technical program that studies which formal axioms suffice for particular theorems.

The practical choice is therefore among representations and methods with
assessable contributions. A richer model is warranted when it resolves a
consequential ambiguity; an approximation is useful when its preserved answers
suffice; deeper physical explanation matters when realization is the question.
A bounded result states both what has been obtained and what its method leaves
open. This makes completion possible without confusing limited computation
with a final account of reality.

## A trajectory ready for shared reflection

The reading environment can now be encountered through an intelligible passage:
a purpose prompted a construction, operation and use revealed different
results, candidates were compared, and a revision was tried and judged. Its
current account can be brief because the relevant history remains retrievable.
Unresolved explanations and the limits of the records remain part of what
the participants know about this passage.

This is the result toward which this part has worked: enough connected living
material to revisit shared activity, rather than a complete biography or a
final personal model. In [Mutual becoming through YinYang and Taiji](yinyang-and-taiji.md), the
participants turn toward that trajectory together. They can ask how each
contributed, what the exchange made possible, what was missed, and what they
want to cultivate further. Finite representation has prepared something for
that encounter to explore; the encounter can in turn change what the next
trajectory needs to retain.
