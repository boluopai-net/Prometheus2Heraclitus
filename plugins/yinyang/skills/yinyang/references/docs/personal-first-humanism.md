# Personal-First Humanism

[Home](../../README.md) · [简体中文](personal-first-humanism.zh-CN.md)

*A person encounters an arrangement made for people in general. Something
important in their life has no place in it. Making that difference intelligible
can begin the work of changing the arrangement.*

A philosophy for personal practice begins somewhere concrete: someone
perceives, understands, desires, makes, and lives through the consequences.
Categories such as user, worker, reader, and citizen make some relations
available. The person also brings circumstances and possibilities that those
categories may leave unexpressed. **Personal-first humanism** names an ethical
orientation for developing arrangements from that concrete encounter.

Its practical premise is explicit: people's developing understanding, voluntary
agency, and ability to sustain worthwhile lives should give tools and cooperative
arrangements their direction. A personal philosophy can help articulate what
that premise means here, while a model helps give it working form. Actual
consequences can then revise the arrangement and its account of the person.

## Real persons, revisable descriptions

Taking the person seriously does not require an exhaustive metaphysical theory
of personhood. It requires acknowledging the actual life to which a description
answers: embodied conditions, experiences, relations, commitments, and effects
that continue beyond the description. A model of someone can be inadequate
even when it is internally coherent. Their correction can disclose that
inadequacy; subsequent experience can disclose another.

The framework takes the changing natural world as its reality. **Modest
naturalism** names this worldly scope; **contemporary fire monism** articulates
its dynamic unity, in which persons and their relations persist and change.
Thinking, psychological experience, and engineering share this physical ground;
tools require materials and energy, and bodies live through their consequences.
A computational account abstracts relations relevant to the question, while
experience and evidence test their adequacy. Particular realization mechanisms
and complete reduction require their own inquiries. [Physical ground and computational abstraction](world-and-expression.md#physical-ground-and-computational-abstraction)
develops these connected commitments.

“The person is real” therefore has a practical force here. A category, metric,
or story does not exhaust the one who lives through its application. Its
consequences belong in the assessment. The same force applies to other people;
the encounter has more than one center of concern.

## Construction, nominalism, and fiction

The possibility of constructing a concept invites questions about what it
represents and how it is used. A nominalist question concerns the status of
universals and general categories. Hobbes's *Leviathan*, chapter IV, supplies
one historical formulation by treating universal names as applying to
individual things [[1]](#source-1). Such a formulation does not make those particulars
disappear.

Fictionalism raises another question: could a discourse be useful without
accepting its apparent claims as literally true? A selected fictional role
can organize conduct; a thought experiment can develop consequences under
imagined conditions. Their occurrence and effects are actual even when the
represented situation is invented. The [metaphor essay](metaphor-and-self-revision.md)
develops how expressive constructions become interpretations and realizations.

These distinctions leave room to consider the stronger conditional: **if many
of the concepts through which we organize life are constructed or treated as
fictions, which constructions should we choose?** That question makes their
adequacy important. It does not establish that everything is fiction. Nor does
it derive an ethical answer from an ontological label. A construction could
serve administration, efficiency, conformity, discovery, or an individual's
development. Choosing among these directions requires reasons about what
should matter.

The personal-first premise supplies such a reason: concepts and arrangements
should support actual persons' understanding and agency, and remain open to
their corrections. Constructibility gives revision room; the ethical premise
gives revision a direction. The two contributions should remain distinguishable.

## Four connected axes

This proposal can be positioned through four questions. Their working names
articulate an orientation, not a completed universal system:

| Axis | Working orientation | What it asks of the practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ontology | Modest naturalism and contemporary fire monism: one changing natural world as physical ground, with concrete persons and relations within it. | Which objects and relations must the account acknowledge, and what resists its description? |
| Epistemology | Fallible inquiry beginning with embodied experience and corrected through feedback. | Which observations support or challenge an interpretation, and what distinguishes alternatives? |
| Value | Personal-first humanism. | Whose understanding, agency, and worthwhile activity does the arrangement support? |
| Methodology | Question-relative pluralism. | Which combination of analogy, formalization, simulation, participation, and experiment answers this question? |

These four axes use the same distinctions as [Core view YY-09](core-views.md#yy-09). Value articulates what is worth pursuing; an ontological description, factual judgment, or effective method cannot simply replace it. **Practice** brings all four into action and encounters their consequences. A diagram may give practice a fifth node to show this connection without making it a fifth philosophical position of the same kind.

These questions also need to be developed separately at physical and computational
levels. The physical level concerns embodied activity and its material conditions;
the computational level concerns usable objects, relations, and organization of
activity. Both levels are real, and philosophical understanding applies to both:

| Dimension | Physical level | Computational level |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ontology | Physical ontology: how do embodied persons, machines, and material processes exist, persist, and change? | Computational ontology: a process ontology organizing events, states, objects, relations, and transitions. |
| Knowing | How do experience and observation support judgment and disclose error? | How are evidence, candidate explanations, inference, and tests represented and organized? |
| Value | What is worth pursuing; whose agency and life are affected? | How are goals, criteria, constraints, and tradeoffs made explicit? |
| Method | How are inquiry, collaboration, and experiments actually conducted? | How do method descriptions, procedures, tools, and rules organize selected activity? |
| Practice | How do embodied people act and live with consequences? | How are computational arrangements run and used, and their results read back? |

[From understanding the world to realizing a practice](../diagrams/layers.html)
draws these two sets of four axes plus practice as two levels, naming their
ontology nodes **physical ontology** and **computational ontology**. Process
ontology at the computational level organizes persistence and change; it cannot
replace that level's responsibilities of knowing, value, and method. Bodies,
machines, records, energy, and activity at the physical level realize the
computational arrangement.
The corresponding dimensions instead connect through selection, expression,
organization, and feedback. A value commitment guides goal design; it is not
the material that makes a program. Both levels belong to one world, and
computational activity is real activity. Placing the physical level below the
computational level depicts realization, not a restriction of reality to the lower level.

Evidence representations therefore remain answerable to experience, evaluation
criteria to lived purposes, and executed procedures to judgments of methodological
fit. A well-formed representation can explain the phenomenon poorly; a predictive
model can serve a purpose we have reason to reject. Articulating both sets
separately makes their actual connections open to examination.

“Begin with psychological adequacy before formal refinement” expresses a useful
research direction when the question concerns a psychological phenomenon:
recognize what needs explanation, then let formal tools clarify and test the
candidate structure. The activities can interleave. Adequacy means addressing
the phenomenon, including differences that an interpretation fails to explain.
It does not mean making someone comfortable through flattering agreement.
Mathematical validity and empirical adequacy retain their distinct checks.

Preferences can choose a question, direct attention, or guide the adoption of
a working view. Evidence constrains what can be accepted as true about its
object. Intuition and abduction can propose a candidate before all its conditions
are explicit; comparison and feedback can develop or defeat it. This is why
the epistemological name above emphasizes a starting point and corrigibility.

## Adapt arrangements around people

A personal-first design asks how a difficulty is produced before asking the
person to absorb it. Some difficulties can be changed through a cooperative
arrangement: when work happens, how decisions are shared, or what others need
to receive. Others call for different software or an interface. A person can
also choose to learn, practice, or change a habit for an end they recognize.

Considering social arrangements and software before demanding personal
adaptation is a productive direction. It opens alternatives that a fixed
environment might conceal. Actual dependencies can require another order;
the question is which change serves the purpose under the circumstances.

For instance, an explanatory assistant might send every investigative detail
to the reader. Instead of treating the reader's overload as a defect to train
away, the collaborators can redesign the explanation around the judgment the
reader needs to make. The model can carry retrieval and implementation while
making reasons, alternatives, and material uncertainty intelligible. The
person participates through recognition, questioning, and decisions that matter.
If the purpose is learning the investigative method, the allocation can change.

The improvement should be assessed through actual work: can the person
understand the choice, influence its direction, and use what is produced?
Output volume and the model's reassurance are different observations.

## Society is an encounter among persons

Society includes institutions and norms that shape what people can do. It also
includes other persons with their own purposes, needs, and contributions.
A philosophy more adequate to one individual can improve shared arrangements
by revealing an overlooked possibility. It can also impose costs on others.
Those effects belong to its assessment.

Mill's *On Liberty*, chapter III, argues for individuality and different
experiments of living while retaining the condition of not injuring others [[2]](#source-2).
This offers a source for the connection between singular development and
plural coexistence. The present proposal develops it through revisable tools
and model-supported practices.

A common framework can enable people to cooperate without demanding identical
lives. Standards, stable interfaces, and shared procedures can protect useful
continuity; they should also be open to criticism where their effects defeat
their purposes. A person's disagreement is an occasion to investigate the
relation, not automatic evidence that every social constraint is harmful.

Models contribute to that inquiry as interpreting and generating counterparts.
Their participation can enlarge human agency while retaining independent
judgment. Personal-first humanism chooses a purpose for the arrangement;
it does not require the person to author every idea or perform every step.
The composition in [Sun, Moon, and Earth](yinyang-and-taiji.md#sun-moon-and-earth) keeps these contributions
and the independent world together.

## A changing person in a changing world

A person's understanding develops while tools and social conditions can also
change. A representation adequate to an earlier encounter may need revision
because the question, capabilities, or conditions have changed. Preserving
continuity means retaining what still serves the practice while making such
differences recognizable.

Carnap's account of framework adoption attends to purpose, fruitfulness, and
the effort involved in using a linguistic form [[3]](#source-3). It provides a comparison
for this choice among representations. [Embodied Experimental Philosophy](embodied-experimental-philosophy.md)
connects their selection with participation and later evidence. A system can
support reasoning; reflection can also examine its boundary and aim. Dynamic
representation and model selection are resources in this work, rather than
proof that any one account contains the whole life.

Return to the person whose life had no place in a general arrangement.
Success need not mean that the person has learned to fit the old category.
An explanation may become intelligible, a tool usable, or a different way
of cooperating sustainable. The person may also choose a demanding change
in themselves. Humanism gives these transformations their measure: what
understanding, voluntary agency, and worthwhile activity do they make
possible, and what do they ask of other lives?

## Sources and scope

The connected naturalist and fire-monist account, the four-axis positioning, and their model-supported
realization are this essay's synthesis. The sources contribute the distinctions
identified above; the ethical orientation is argued here and remains open to
criticism and inquiry through its consequences.

1. <a id="source-1"></a> Thomas Hobbes (1651), **Leviathan**, chapter IV, “Of Speech.” [Original text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Leviathan_%281651%29/Chapter_4). One historical account of universal names and particular things, not a proof of the present ethical orientation.
2. <a id="source-2"></a> John Stuart Mill (1859), **On Liberty**, chapter III. [Original text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/34901/34901-h/34901-h.htm). Individuality and experiments of living within plural coexistence.
3. <a id="source-3"></a> Rudolf Carnap (1950), **Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology**. [Public reprint](https://ditext.com/carnap/carnap.html). Purpose-sensitive adoption of linguistic frameworks; the personal-first premise is supplied separately here.
