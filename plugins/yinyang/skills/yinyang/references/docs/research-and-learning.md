# Research and Learning

**Most of the time, attend to the whole; some of the time, develop familiarity with important points and details, and know when to examine them.** Begin with a field's questions, concepts, and relationships, then deepen particular parts as needed. Prioritize broadly applicable understanding and methods.

Design is one example: deciding what to make, why, how to organize it, and what would count as a fitting result. When AI carries implementation, people can concentrate their learning on these questions. Designing twice or more means generating and comparing alternatives, reconsidering the problem, premises, and evaluation criteria when needed. Even the framework organizing practice and reflection can change; this is the sense of [reasoning beyond the current double loop](https://boluopai.net/philosophy/embodied-experimental-philosophy/en/) used here.

## First distinguish what you want to learn

If you want to **give yourself a university education, or fill important gaps in foundational and practical knowledge**, the five steps below provide a systematic route: draft a curriculum from your interests, prepare courses, books, tools, and an environment, then write theory and practice paths and begin learning. You can cover one important field or gradually develop your own curriculum without copying every requirement of a degree.

Another entrance begins with books or papers. Becoming familiar with an unfamiliar field through these alone can be harder to organize: papers often omit shared background, and a book may assume foundations from other disciplines. Models can help reconstruct the missing knowledge relationships, while the purpose of reading determines how far to pursue them.

| Current purpose | Where to begin | How far to understand |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Systematically develop foundational and practical knowledge | The five steps below, covering courses, materials, environment, and learning paths | Connect the field's knowledge structure and develop familiarity with relevant abilities through actual use. |
| Enter research literature around a question | [Follow a target paper's references back to foundations](#paper-reading), then compare neighboring questions and methods | Understand relevant conclusions, conditions, and basic arguments or proofs well enough to begin comparing research. |
| Learn in everyday life and broaden understanding | [Select important abstractions and conclusions from a book or paper](#everyday-learning) | Understand the useful perspective, the conditions of its conclusions, and what it helps you understand. |

These entrances can connect; they are not three stages everyone must complete. Research reading can reveal a substantial gap that calls for a course, while everyday reading can uncover a question worth pursuing. **The materials, environment, and path preparation below belongs to the systematic course route; reading one paper does not require building an entire curriculum first.** Either teacher can support these entrances: [choose the convenient teacher](#learning) for the current activity.

## Shared support: reference materials and teachers

For a systematic course, a book, a single paper, or a question pursued across several papers, give the source and learning purpose to **Dot or local Codex**. Have the model use a **Teacher Skill** to write connected reference materials, then work with a teacher to read, explain, and discuss them. **Preparing materials and using a Teacher support all these learning entrances; they are not exclusive to course preparation.**

Depending on the learning object, use the two- or three-document arrangement below. The model explains concepts and relationships, retains chapter, lecture, page, or paper-passage locators, adds examples and useful diagrams, and connects existing understanding, interests, and actual questions:

| Material | Mathematics and technical study: three connected documents | Philosophical reading: two connected documents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Glossary | Keep concepts, notation, conditions, and important distinctions independently accessible, with a concept map showing relationships. | Explain terms in their author's, period's, and argument's context, connecting them through a concept map. |
| Main guide | A study and research guide develops the knowledge structure, principles, derivations, examples, reading order, and connections to your questions. | A reading guide develops problems, claims, reasons, objections, and responses, preserving interpretive differences and relevant connections. |
| Exercises and experiments | Distinguish written exercises from experimentation or making; keep questions, solutions, and evaluation grounds separately readable, with links to the guide and glossary. | Do not automatically add technical labs. Discussion, interpretation, comparison, and counterexamples can develop through reading; prepare technical training when such an activity is actually selected. |

Organize materials around one learning object. A single paper can have its own two or three reference documents; papers addressing one question can share a set while retaining the relevant locations in each source. Do not mechanically duplicate it for every video, slide deck, or supporting citation. Overall learning paths select across courses; a specific guide develops understanding of the current book, paper, or question. Original sources and model explanations retain their separate identities.

Preparation depth follows purpose. A systematic course can have its full materials prepared before study begins. Single-paper reading and research learning can start with the materials currently needed, then add foundational papers' knowledge and relationships to the glossary and guide as gaps emerge. Everyday learning can use the same arrangement, emphasizing important abstractions, conclusions, and examples, with a few understanding checks where helpful rather than automatically expanding into comprehensive training.

## 1. Draft a curriculum from your interests

### Explore interests and compare curricula

Begin with an interesting direction, such as human factors. Discuss with AI whether you want to understand cognition, human–machine cooperation, or the design of products and working environments. Concrete questions can gradually clarify the direction.

Ask AI to investigate Chinese universities with established work in that direction and find **recent master's curricula and entrance-examination reading lists for the relevant programs**. Related material may appear under ergonomics, engineering psychology, or human–computer interaction; judge the actual research groups and courses. Extract courses, topics, prerequisites, and books, retaining the university, department, year, and original link.

Where programs emphasize engineering, psychology, or design, or sit in computer science, psychology, artificial intelligence, design, or other departments, select representative curricula. Have AI explain the questions, knowledge, and methods each emphasizes.

### Draft a course system around one main curriculum

Ask AI to judge which curriculum fits your interests, responses to courses, and intended activities, explaining its reasons so you can choose or revise the judgment. **Use the most interesting curriculum as the main structure and fill gaps from the others**: merge duplicates, add missing foundations and methods, arrange prerequisites, and distinguish core courses from interest-led extensions. Each course should have a clear purpose and relationship to the others.

The result at this point is an adjustable curriculum draft. As materials and tools are prepared, record which courses, books, and practical abilities can connect; organize the learning paths once those resources are in place. Preparation may reveal an uninteresting course, unsuitable materials, or changed tool conditions. Return to replace a course, revise the curriculum, or choose a more interesting direction when needed.

## 2. Turn courses and books into readable materials

### Find matching resources and download them

Search primarily for **online courses from outside China with matching topics or course names, selecting the latest available versions**. Check syllabi and teaching years; explain content matches across different names and identify older versions when those are all that is available. Also find Chinese entrance-examination books and books named in curricula or course materials.

Have the model download all available resources it finds, including videos, notes, slides, subtitles, assignments, code, and books. Organize them by course and check completeness. Where the model cannot complete a download, list the material, original link, and reason for manual completion. Distinguish downloaded materials, online-only entrances, and outstanding items in the index.

### Prepare course materials with the shared method

Use the [shared reference-material arrangement](#learning-materials) to prepare a glossary, main guide, and relevant exercises or experiments for the selected courses. Where training materials are useful, prepare questions, solutions, and evaluation grounds across the course's scope, checking answers and keeping them separate from questions for later selection. This systematic course route prepares materials, an environment, and learning paths before reading and practice; other reading entrances can develop materials during reading.

## 3. Prepare practical tools and an environment

After preparing materials and notes, have the model connect the environment needed by the curriculum draft, acquired resources, and abilities you want to develop. Software may be installed for later use or operated with model assistance; a complex interface or current unfamiliarity is not by itself a reason to exclude it. Installation, payment, usable capability, and personal familiarity are separate judgments.

| What to prepare | Contribution to learning |
| --- | --- |
| Software and materials | Drawing, prototypes, audio and video, 3D, statistics, or experiments, with usable files, assets, and save locations. |
| Application plugins and resources | Import/export, components, charts, animation, or specialist operations, checked against the current application version. |
| Skills and teaching methods | Guidance for the model's explanation, production, and checking in this kind of work. |
| MCP and account connections | Actual access to the necessary documents, design files, data, or application operations, verified with a real read or edit. |
| CLIs, scripts, and interface operation | Command-line, programmatic, or computer-interface routes appropriate to the application. A tool without MCP may still be usable through another available route. |

Not every tool needs all of these entrances. Have the model inspect existing applications, plugins, and public interfaces, select a combination that serves the current work, and verify it with a small real operation: read the material, perform the action, save the result, reopen it, and continue. Successful installation or a “connected” label does not establish that the whole practice path works.

For paid tools, first understand licensing, subscriptions, and feature limits; preparing software does not itself authorize a purchase. When retaining a fixed version matters, record the usable version, disable its automatic updates, and preserve recoverable installation materials. Select versions for actual compatibility and use rather than following every update.

Retain the tool capabilities, usable materials, and operation entrances established during preparation for the next step of writing the paths. Once practice begins, return what the tool is useful for, how to express requirements, how to assess results, and what you want to learn next to the practice path. The model can maintain software inventories and installation records; the human reading entrance should explain possible activities, their course connections, and how to begin. Model-facing collaboration accounts and personal materials may support this arrangement, but do not replace a readable learning path or require publication of personal records.

<a id="learning-paths"></a>

## 4. Write the theory and practice learning paths

Once the materials and practical environment are ready, have the model organize the connections developed during preparation into two usable paths. The theory path should point to acquired courses, books, notes, and passages; the practice path should point to available tools, materials, operation entrances, and abilities to develop. The earlier curriculum draft supplies direction; this step supplies grounded, specific learning paths.

**The theory path organizes knowledge; the practice path organizes abilities and tools.** A course usually includes both theory and practice, but these forms of organization answer different questions. Courses help explain concepts, principles, and methods; reading alone does not develop the ability to design, analyze, or make things. Difficulties encountered in practice reveal which theories deserve closer study. Practice need not wait until every course is complete.

| Path | What it organizes | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Theory learning path | Questions in the field, course modules, conceptual relationships, prerequisites, and readings | Develop an overall understanding and select courses or passages for deeper inquiry. |
| Practice learning path | Abilities in expression and judgment, tool use, making or experimentation, checking results, and revision | Begin with an ability you want to develop, practice with an actual object, and return to relevant theory. |

Write the practice path as an **outline of abilities and tools**, rather than replacing a whole direction with one invented project. Design might distinguish paper sketches, visual expression, information and interaction structure, prototypes, motion, platform differences, and design handoff. Statistics might distinguish data preparation, visualization, model selection, interpretation, and checking. These are capability areas; the particular work, dataset, or task used for practice can be chosen when learning actually takes place.

For employment, have the model examine real job requirements and work materials, separating common foundations, specialties, experience requirements, and alternative tools. For curiosity, creation, or research, select from the relevant works, questions, and methods. One posting, a software list, or a model's imagination cannot establish a compulsory training program for everyone.

Keep an existing curriculum when it fits, adding the practice path and its connections to courses. Paths support selection and continuity without becoming fixed daily quotas. Check that both paths lead to the corresponding materials and usable environment; return any gaps to preparation, then begin reading and practice. Interests and difficulties encountered during learning can reopen the paths and their preparation.

<a id="understanding-and-evaluation"></a>

## 5. Read, practice, and examine important points

Begin actual study after preparing the materials, environment, and paths. During preparation the model organizes and checks resources and tests tools; the person need not start the course reading or exercises described below. **Once everything is ready, open it when you feel good and want to study.**

Choose the convenient teacher through the [shared teacher entrance](#learning), then begin with the prepared materials.

### Begin with the current question

Open the main guide to see what the unit develops and how it relates to what precedes and follows it. Follow an unfamiliar term into the glossary, then return to the passage. Open the cited source when its meaning, derivation, or evidence needs checking. The guide is more than an index of original passages, and the glossary need not be memorized before reading.

Use diagrams to see relationships and discussion to express your understanding and questions. Ask the teacher to connect new and existing knowledge, compare explanations, and develop what remains unclear, then check whether it misunderstood. Every encounter need not interrupt reading for exhaustive study of details. Saved notes and current explanations can revise one another: add useful examples, explanations, and new questions to the existing materials rather than accumulating hard-to-find copies.

### Develop theoretical understanding and practical familiarity together

Theory follows questions, concepts, and reasons. Practice selects a useful ability from the outline and makes it recognizable and actionable through real materials, tools, and feedback. After studying motion principles, observe and compare transitions, then make and revise a response. After understanding a statistical model, use real or clearly identified teaching data to check whether inputs, outputs, and interpretation correspond. Actual tasks can change without making the whole learning path depend on one project.

Tool familiarity includes knowing what a tool can do, how files and operations are organized, when to use it, how to express an intention, and how to judge the result. Models can operate complex interfaces, write code, and carry repetitive production; people can participate through paper sketches, photographs, speech, comparison, and revision. Preserve hands-on attempts when developing your own drawing or direct-operation ability. Where a job or examination requires personal performance, check against that requirement. Do not count the model's contribution as your independent mastery.

### When to examine important points and details

Return to relevant concepts, conditions, and evidence when a detail could change the overall judgment, results depart from expectations, understanding becomes inconsistent, or a method is entering a new context. Identify the specific difficulty with the teacher, then use examples, counterexamples, reasoning, or experiments to examine it. For example, moving from correlation to a causal explanation calls for identifying the additional assumptions and whether evidence supports them.

**Actual practice should exclude most prepared material, retaining only the very few points essential now.** Add diagrams, concrete examples, and smaller exercises at those points; repeat or change contexts when needed until understanding supports the current judgment, then return to the whole. Unselected materials remain available for later needs or review after forgetting.

Practice develops understanding; testing supplies performance evidence; evaluation uses that evidence to decide what needs improvement. Explanations, responses to counterexamples, and practical work can also supply evidence without becoming examinations. When judging personal understanding, distinguish your contribution from model assistance: one correct answer or a correct model output alone does not demonstrate mastery.

### Research also requires checking the result

Learning can serve curiosity and understanding without serving research. Research develops a reasoned, testable contribution to an unresolved question and includes continued learning. **Personal understanding and the reliability of research results need separate judgments.** Have the model check citations and claims against original sources, examine premises, calculations, and code, and cross-check through other methods, counterexamples, or reproduction. People need to understand the consequential assumptions, evidence, and limitations to judge whether the result answers the question. Return to the relevant course when a knowledge gap appears.

## Follow papers back to foundations and enter an unfamiliar field

### When a paper is inaccessible, find an understandable starting point through its references

Choose a target paper relevant to your question. Ask the model to explain its problem, contribution, and the concepts or inferences blocking understanding. Replacing difficult wording with simpler words is insufficient when background knowledge is missing. Have the model inspect the references and search backward for more foundational papers explaining those concepts, results, or methods. A citation is not automatically a prerequisite: inspect the material to determine whether it supplies a more accessible foundation. A textbook chapter or review can provide a bridge where needed.

Begin with material you can connect to what you already understand. Grasp its **question, main conclusions, conditions, and the basic argument or proof supporting them**. Return to the paper that uses it and explain how this newly understood knowledge supports the next step. If a gap remains, look further back; when the current dependency is understood sufficiently, work forward toward the target. The model can diagram these relationships and retain original passages so you can see why each paper is being read and what it contributes, without traversing every reference.

Proceed through the needed concepts until the original target paper becomes understandable, then move to another related paper. Neighboring work introduces new concepts, different methods, and limits to previous conclusions. Repeat the process for the new knowledge that matters. Understanding the necessary surrounding literature means covering the key dependencies, representative methods, and important disputes for the current question well enough to understand and compare them. Expand when a new question requires it, rather than attempting to exhaust the field's literature.

### Move from important questions toward methods that interest you

The model can also help assess the **importance and feasibility** of the target paper and related research directions. Why does its problem matter? What does it contribute beyond existing work, and what remains unresolved? What knowledge, data, equipment, time, or collaboration would you need to participate? Tie these judgments to papers, reviews, and actual resources, separating established facts from conjecture. An important field does not make every problem in it suitable for you to work on now.

Importance and interest can guide different reading choices. An important paper may address a worthwhile question through a method you do not want to study deeply. Understand its question and contribution, then ask the model to find **other treatments of the same or neighboring problems**. Compare what each method reveals, its costs, and what it leaves unresolved. These connections can lead from a paper whose method does not appeal to you toward one using a method you prefer. Return to foundational reading when gaps prevent understanding it.

A preferred method supplies motivation to learn; its fit to a problem still depends on assumptions, evidence, and results. Retain important work that could change your judgment even when it does not match your interests, without reading every paper equally deeply. Models can retrieve, organize, explain, and compare. If you go on to make research claims, modify a method, or test results, deepen the learning into the relevant critical proofs, experiments, and counterexamples rather than stopping at a summary of conclusions.

<a id="everyday-learning"></a>

## Everyday learning: understand important abstractions and conclusions

If your purpose is everyday learning and broader understanding, reading an unfamiliar field's papers can usually focus on **valuable, important abstractions and final conclusions**. You need not train as a researcher in that field, reproduce the whole study, or master every proof. Ask the model what phenomena the paper represents through which objects and relationships, why that abstraction is useful, what conclusions it reaches, and how these connect to things you already know.

To avoid retaining a memorable but easily misapplied conclusion, also understand its key conditions, scope, and limitations. Use a concrete example and a case where it does not apply to check understanding. Technical details that do not serve the current purpose can be left aside, while keeping the authors' findings, the model's explanation, and your own associations distinguishable. This achieves understanding of important ideas and conclusions without claiming an independent ability to evaluate the reliability of the entire study.

When an abstraction is especially fruitful, or you intend to make a practical decision from it, examine the corresponding argument, evidence, and foundations more closely. Everyday reading can already be a complete learning purpose; it need not automatically become a research plan or a curriculum.
