# Reasoning within an unfinished stream

[简体中文](reasoning.zh-CN.md)

A trajectory of shared work becomes intelligible when we can recover more
than its latest conclusion. What question were the participants trying to
answer, which alternatives did they consider, what did they try, what happened,
and why did their judgment change? [Forming a life trajectory](flow-and-time.md)
explains how traces connect earlier activity with the present. This essay
develops their organization as an **inquiry history**, in which a conclusion
remains connected with its grounds and the path by which it was reached.

The reading environment introduced in [World, process, and expression](world-and-expression.md)
provides a continuing example. “Make access convenient” first directs shared
construction. A tidy shelf is implemented, yet the person reports difficulty
resuming a passage. The inquiry must now distinguish what was built from what
was wanted, compare explanations, and try a revision. Retaining this movement
makes later reflection possible: a finished interface alone cannot show how
the person and counterpart learned what convenient access meant here.

Inquiry can begin with an image, tension, question, or tendency to act before
a proposition is available. Articulation makes selected relations open to
discussion and checking. Its development into candidates and local judgments
is itself part of the trajectory.
YinYang's worldview places these activities within one changing world; its
ontology asks how objects form, persist, and change. A **working computational
ontology** explicitly expresses the objects, events, states, relations, and
transitions needed for a question, for the model to interpret, reason with, and
use. Process ontology guides these distinctions here. They can describe actual
psychological activity or develop hypothetical situations and fictional objects,
supporting simulation where useful. Person and language model form the question and this
model together, divide explicit reasoning, construction, and observation by
their actual capabilities, and revise through the same continuing exchange.
[Natural world and computational abstraction](world-and-expression.md#physical-ground-and-computational-abstraction)
develops representation and its physical grounding. Here the question is
how to form and test such models, rather than define computation by a method
of hypothesis formation.

## Process and current view

An abductive–predictive stream accommodates material that does not yet fit together. A discrepancy may suggest a hidden relation; candidate explanations organize it; those explanations yield conditional predictions; observation distinguishes, supports, or revises them. Several explanations can remain available, including mutually exclusive or incomparable ones. Conflict opens a question without deciding which explanation is true. Context, time, and representational differences can also explain an apparent conflict, and inquiry need not always begin with one.

A **working view** is a usable understanding formed for the present question: what is being explained, which grounds are provisionally adopted, which candidates remain under comparison, and how the participants intend to infer, try, or check something. It can be expressed in a few paragraphs, a diagram, or an explicit knowledge base. Participants construct it in their situation. Discovering a relation or reformulating the question can change it, beyond selecting content from an existing archive. Papers, diagrams, speech, and records retain articulated relations so that this construction can return to its grounds.

The working view connects with the [generative description](literary-recursive-improvement.md#body-generative-description-and-interpreter): philosophy, collaborative direction, and revision methods help the participants understand how to work here; the current view brings them to a particular question. A reading task can take “preserve the thread of thought” as its direction, then determine which passage to return to and what would establish resumed reading. Differences encountered in use can in turn change the more enduring description. The distinction concerns their functions; the same passage can perform one or both functions in different activities.

Articulation changes what can be checked. A sense that a result is wrong may
first become a question about its destination, then a candidate explanation
of how it was produced. Each formulation opens some checks and leaves others
outside its scope. Inquiry must therefore assess its formulation alongside
the answers it obtains. The reading-environment example below makes this movement concrete.

## Relations before sentences

Intuition is more than a starting point before candidate generation. It participates in recognizing, filtering, combining, and rejecting during development, allowing some possibilities to continue while others leave the current view. Preference affects what deserves attention; experience and reasons contribute to judging what those possibilities explain. Broad reading and embodied perception supply new relations, so selection and generation can propel each other. Several meaningful candidates can remain open to examination. Understanding their consequential differences gives a present choice its content.

Sufficient exploration is relative to the question and its consequences: whether important alternatives that could change the judgment have become visible, which disagreements need further material, and when there is enough support to act. This gives exploration a direction and a stopping condition. Formalization contributes when relations need clarification, consequences need verification, or a work needs realization. An initial unfolding of relations gives it something meaningful to articulate and check. [Why organize participation this way](participation-and-support.md#why-organize-participation-this-way) explains how this arrangement serves the cultivation of intuition, the senses, and broad reading.

A relationship can become recognizable through an analogy, scene, image, or sense of mismatch before a complete sentence is available. Expression unfolds it through return, correction, and changes of vocabulary. A fragment can carry several developing relations, and different reconstructions can bring different parts into focus. Fidelity is assessed by returning to the object, context, and purpose that the expression is trying to articulate.

“Neural generation and symbolic constraint” names a functional analogy. Contextual generation can disclose possibilities, judge them, reject them, and revise them. Explicit representations make assumptions, provenance, priorities, conflicts, and proofs inspectable. Statistical assessment addresses uncertainty and fit; logical checking exposes invalid connections. These functions can overlap within either participant's contribution. The distinction helps organize their cooperation according to what a particular inquiry needs.

Logic contributes a callable checking capacity. Truth-seeking, corrigibility, relevant evidence, and avoiding arbitrary consequences guide developed factual arguments even when their representation changes. A chosen value orientation can supply a starting point for expression and action: a person may explore what follows from valuing autonomy or cultivation. Its consequences can be articulated before the orientation has been justified to every possible audience. Factual claims made along the way remain answerable to evidence, and inferential claims to their premises and connections. Checking begins with whether the reconstruction concerns the expressed object and purpose, then asks what kind of support its conclusion calls for.

## Whitehead: importance, expression, and revising a perspective

Whitehead's *Modes of Thought* offers a useful connection. In [Lecture I, sections 3–6](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Whitehead/Whitehead_1938/1938_01.html), a finite intellect selects through relative importance, and a perspective grades relevance. Importance and factuality require one another: attending to a fact already gives it a place in an activity of concern. This helps explain why choosing what matters is not itself a claim that other possibilities are false. Whitehead treats importance as a fundamental notion; reducing it to a formula such as preference plus rational analysis would lose that scope.

In [Lecture II, section 1](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Whitehead/Whitehead_1938/1938_02.html), expression carries a finite occasion's perspective into its environment. Selection also belongs to expression. What becomes public therefore need not reproduce every possibility entertained: a statement, diagram, or action gives selected relations a definite form. Expression is broader than verbal explanation or deductive consequence; these are particular ways a person can make something available to others.

[Lecture III, sections 1–3 and 6–7](https://brocku.ca/MeadProject/Whitehead/Whitehead_1938/1938_03.html) presents understanding as incomplete penetration into relationships. It can grow through details within an existing pattern or through a new pattern that makes previously neglected details relevant. Proof begins from premises whose evidence is already presupposed. This gives a basis for examining the abstractions organizing an inquiry as well as reasoning within them. Understanding can deepen a retained perspective; it need not change the selection on every occasion.

YinYang develops this connection as **accommodating possibilities and selecting by importance; articulating and checking consequences; returning to revise the selection and its premises**. These contributions overlap and can recur. Interests, values, and reasoned comparison participate in selecting a working view; analysis can subsequently change what seems important. A new understanding may reopen an omitted relation, alter a priority, or reconstruct the premise that made the earlier alternatives seem exhaustive. This connects with [the dialectical reconstruction of a question](speech-opposition-and-self-formation.md#conflicting-models-and-reconstructing-the-question), without identifying that method with Whitehead's philosophy or equating the whole of understanding with reselection.

An expression judged better across several perspectives needs grounds for that comparison. Logical entailment establishes what follows under specified premises and interpretation; it does not establish effectiveness in most real situations. The relevant situations, purposes, and empirical support still need examination. Retaining multiple views can make a conclusion less dependent on one selection, while their shared omissions remain revisable. This is the difference between giving a choice intelligible consequences and treating its importance as a substitute for evidence.

The [learning practice](research-and-learning.md#understanding-and-evaluation) supplies a concrete example. Broad preparation keeps questions and alternatives available. Actual practice removes most of that coverage, selects very few points by their present importance, and strengthens them through examples, diagrams, and repeated checks. Feedback may change which points matter, returning the participants to the wider material or to a different organization of the question. Selection concentrates attention without exhausting what can later become meaningful.

## A working ontology forms with its question

A working ontology articulates the domain concepts, relations, and constraints
that matter for a question, including relevant persistence and change. Process
ontology supplies an interpretive direction; the question determines which
distinctions need development. Person, counterpart, unfinished work, correction,
and continuation can, for example, acquire explicit natural-language relations
within a private sinthome for the model to use in subsequent work.
[Forming a sinthome](writing-a-sinthome.md#computational-ontology-in-a-private-sinthome)
develops that use. Its concepts
can be introduced, weakened, replaced, or reorganized. A computer consumer may
use distinctions carried in clear natural language or require a more formal
representation. Their meanings need to survive that use; factual claims remain
answerable to what is actually the case, while inference develops consequences
under the chosen interpretation and premises. Either participant can examine
both. Merely retaining a vocabulary does not achieve this. More
definitions can increase both expressive power and the cost of changing the
account.

Economy asks added structure to contribute to explanation, prediction, or action. A “minimal sufficient model” expresses this preference for restrained complexity under the question's actual evidence. Sufficiency is assessed through the question it answers and the consequences it makes available; different models may serve different uses. A premise that simply restates the desired conclusion may make an inference valid while contributing little explanation. [Finite inquiry and abstraction](finite-inquiry-and-abstraction.md) distinguishes representation cost from metaphysical commitment and ultimate reduction.

Changing a question can change its ontology. Asking whether a program executed
an operation requires distinctions among calls, states, and outputs. Asking
whether it accomplished a purpose also requires the intended result and its
conditions of use. The first answer can be correct while leaving the second
question unanswered. A useful reformulation makes this added relation explicit
and checks which earlier conclusions still apply.

## Sources, candidates, and the active workspace

| Position | What it retains | What revision changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Utterances, documents, actions, measurements, and their provenance. | Appraisal and annotation can change without replacing the original occurrence with an interpretation. |
| Candidate | Competing explanations or models formed from sources. | Evidence can alter, compare, repair, or defeat them. |
| Active workspace | The sources, candidates, concepts, and checks used for the current question. | Attention can switch, prune, unload, or later recover them. |

Attention, retention, and acceptance have different effects. Removing a candidate from current use frees the workspace while leaving its source and possible later reconsideration available. Retaining incompatible explanations preserves alternatives for comparison; accepting their claims together as classical premises would expose the inference to contradiction. A source can be worth retaining while its explanation is still developing. The present question determines which branches are worth pursuing.

The working view makes the commitments needed for this question explicit while the wider source material remains available, including incompatible reports and explanations. A later result can change the selected premises, the question, or the way candidates are generated and evaluated. [Recursive improvement](literary-recursive-improvement.md#from-rewriting-words-to-changing-the-next-activity) follows how a revised method enters subsequent activity through interpretation and realization.

An inquiry history preserves these differences across time. An abandoned branch
can retain its question and reason for abandonment without remaining an active
instruction or an accepted conclusion. Returning to it may explain an earlier
choice or expose a condition that has changed. The current account states what
the participants now judge; the retained trajectory makes that judgment's
formation open to examination.

Before comparing conflicting candidates, establish whether their assertions
concern the same object, time, and conditions. Yesterday's failed run and
today's successful run can both be accurately reported. [Forming a life trajectory](flow-and-time.md)
develops how retained reports bear on a present judgment.

Optional native memory can support these distinctions without settling their judgments.
In the [reading-environment example](flow-and-time.md#shared-work-produces-the-trajectory),
a retained report, an observed page change, and an explanation of reading friction
have different grounds. Hindsight facts retain attributed content; observations
consolidate revisable interpretations; question-defined pages develop accounts
for use. A generated account remains answerable to originals and current checks.
Six sibling answers sharing those sources are coordinated interpretations, not
six independent witnesses to the explanation. The distinction between selecting
a working view and establishing its grounds survives the change of tool.

## Seeking explanations and comparing consequences

Abduction seeks possible explanatory structures from observations; deduction develops consequences within a proposed structure. A response, failure, or unexpected result can indicate how a system filters input, which distinctions it uses, or which assumption is missing. Several explanations may predict the same response. A single observation rarely identifies a unique internal mechanism, and an interaction can change the conditions it is probing.

Return to the hypothetical reading environment. The model reports that access
works because a test opens the selected book. The person still cannot resume
reading conveniently. One candidate is an execution defect: perhaps the
interface opens the wrong destination. Another is a mismatch in the question:
opening a book was checked, but returning to the relevant passage was wanted.
An operation trace and inspection of the displayed result can check the first;
the person's use and clarification bear on the second. If the correct book
opens but requires repeated searching, a successful operation and an unmet
purpose can both be accurately reported. The next version might offer a direct
return to the passage, whose usefulness still needs actual use.

The resulting inquiry history can connect the original request, the two
candidates, the inspected version and opening operation, the person's report,
the changed question, and the revised interface. It also retains that the
execution-defect candidate was considered and, if the check rules it out,
why it was set aside. A later summary saying only “the person prefers direct
links” would lose this history of how the relation was discovered, tested,
and given a working form. The example illustrates what those connections
make recoverable; it reports no actual experiment.

Candidate comparison needs both directions. First reconstruct possible assumptions and relations; then derive consequences that would distinguish them and compare those consequences with observation. An internally coherent story is insufficient. A prediction can fail because of an unsupported premise, an omitted condition, a mistaken reconstruction, or an unsuitable inference method. Locating the failure helps decide what to revise.

Comparing consequences also identifies where support ends. A conclusion
supported by every member of a nonempty admissible candidate family is robust
relative to that family; a shared omission can still defeat it in the world.
When candidates disagree, a further observation may distinguish them, or a
limited conclusion may survive the disagreement. [The Preference Harness](preference-harness.md)
develops the separate choices of which views to consider and what support
to require for acceptance.

The distinction matters because choosing what to investigate is not yet deciding what to believe. A candidate may deserve attention because its consequences are important or easy to test while remaining poorly supported. Conversely, a well-supported local conclusion can be accepted without exhausting every imaginable alternative. Answerability to evidence and finite completion belong together: the view states what it warrants under its conditions, while the stream remains open to later correction.

## Shared scientific inquiry

The movement from retained opposition to a new working unity can require more
than selecting one existing view. If the available views share an inadequate
premise, revise the distinctions and relations that generated the alternatives.
[Conflicting models and reconstructing the question](speech-opposition-and-self-formation.md#conflicting-models-and-reconstructing-the-question)
develops this dialectical comparison; the repair example keeps its exact
inference claims separate from the work of reconstructing a model.

Retaining conflicts and comparing interpretations prepare material for later reflection; they have not already unified the oppositions. When a question concerns how concepts such as independence and assistance organize experience, premises and relationships may themselves need reconstruction. [Speech, opposition, and self-formation](speech-opposition-and-self-formation.md) takes up that question, while source retention and inference checks keep their methodological role here.

Analysis, explanatory hypothesis formation, synthesis, deduction, and empirical assessment can organize shared inquiry. Their relations allow return, overlap, and entry from an existing model. Peirce's discussion of inquiry provides a historical reference for distinguishing explanatory hypotheses, experiential consequences, and assessment: see [the 1908 manuscript, MS 841](https://fromthepage.com/jeffdown1/c-s-peirce-manuscripts/ms-841-1908-a-neglected-argument-text-and-fragments?page=9). These distinctions help ask where a contribution enters and what subsequent assessment it needs.

A human–model collaboration distributes work by contribution and capability. Intuitive generation and recognition can enter anywhere; the model can contribute candidates as well as articulate, compute, investigate, and check. A partial intuition can orient an investigation, and a model's formulation can make its implications visible. Factual support comes from the relevant observations and warranted connections developed through that work.

Learning and accomplishing a task remain distinguishable purposes. A collaboration can create opportunities to generate, recognize, and revise relationships while carrying explicit work through to its intended result. Domain methods determine which participation is useful. External records preserve relevant context for recovery and comparison. The actual allocation grows from the situated collaboration: what the person wants to develop, what each participant can contribute, and what the work needs.

## More than one layer of feedback

An observation, method, formal design, or particular claim can itself become the object of inquiry. A result at one level can prompt revision at another. Relations, constraints, priorities, and causal dependencies have different meanings; representing them as undifferentiated edges loses what inference needs. Speaking order is only one path through the activity.

The reading-environment example makes the object of each return concrete:

| Object reconsidered | What is discovered | How it enters subsequent work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Judgment about the work | The right book opened, but reading did not resume. | Retain the operation result and the reading report separately; revise the judgment that access was sufficiently convenient. |
| Relations organizing the question | Opening a file and recovering an argument are different achievements. | Include reading position and context in the working view, and select what to check anew. |
| Actual arrangement | Returning to the passage may reduce repeated searching. | Change the entrance and examine its effects through use. |
| Method producing improvement | The original check treated one operation as the whole purpose. | In later work, understand the activity to be continued before selecting checks, and examine that method in subsequent tasks. |

Human understanding, model interpretation, available tools, and actual activity
connect these relations. An unarticulated feeling can open a question, while
what has been expressed or can be observed becomes material available to both
participants. New experience can also change habits, attention, and available
capacities; records and textual revisions capture relevant changes within that
activity. Bringing the revision method itself into this return further requires
the rewritten method to affect how later work gathers material, compares, and acts.

Prediction and world-directed feedback connect local views with later activity. New support or mismatch can change a candidate, the question, the concepts, or the grounds selected for judgment. The [preference account](preference-harness.md#returning-to-and-revising-preference) develops how an earlier selection can become visible and open to reconsideration. The basic judgment and its later revision concern connected but different questions.

Versions and branches can preserve the texts and implementations expressing
these understandings, giving an attempt a recoverable, comparable form. Its
meaning also depends on the question, reasons, and consequences of use. A branch
can develop several candidates, and one candidate can emerge across several
records. A view sufficient for its present question can support completion;
new objects, evidence, or methodological problems encountered later can return
the relevant understanding to inquiry.

Reasoning reaches a local result when a conclusion is clear enough to guide
an act and expose its grounds. Its scope indicates what has been answered,
which alternatives remain consequential, and what new evidence would require
reconsideration. The activity can finish without becoming immune to correction.
This leaves a more precise question: how do we choose the views to work with,
and what evidence and inferential rules warrant accepting their conclusions?
The [Preference Harness](preference-harness.md) develops these distinct
contributions of selection and support. In the reading trajectory, that means
retaining both why direct passage access was chosen and what observations
support saying that the revised arrangement actually helped.
