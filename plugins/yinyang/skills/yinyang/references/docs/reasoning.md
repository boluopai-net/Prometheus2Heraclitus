# Reasoning within an unfinished stream

[简体中文](reasoning.zh-CN.md)

How does an unsettled relation become a conclusion that can responsibly guide
action? Inquiry can begin with an image, tension, question, or tendency to act
before a proposition is available. Articulation makes selected relations open
to discussion and checking. This essay follows their development into candidate
explanations, discriminating consequences, and warranted local judgments.
An **ontology** articulates the objects, relations, and transformations relevant
to that inquiry; **computational** means this articulation can be organized
and used to develop consequences. YinYang applies this functional vocabulary
to a person and a generative model working together.

## Process and current view

An abductive–predictive stream accommodates material that does not yet fit together. A discrepancy may suggest a hidden relation; candidate explanations organize it; those explanations yield conditional predictions; observation distinguishes, supports, or revises them. Several explanations can remain available, including mutually exclusive or incomparable ones. Conflict opens a question without deciding which explanation is true. Context, time, and representational differences can also explain an apparent conflict, and inquiry need not always begin with one.

A working view may contain a knowledge base, but the activity that generates, uses, and changes views is broader. A new distinction can emerge before there is a well-formed query. Helping formulate the question is therefore part of inquiry, not merely preparation for an already specified computation. Papers, diagrams, speech, and records retain articulated relations; they do not exhaust the event or everything that remained unarticulated.

Articulation changes what can be checked. A sense that a result is wrong may
first become a question about its destination, then a candidate explanation
of how it was produced. Each formulation opens some checks and leaves others
outside its scope. Inquiry must therefore assess its formulation alongside
the answers it obtains. The program example below makes this movement concrete.

## Relations before sentences

Intuition is more than a starting point before candidate generation. Through preference it recognizes, filters, combines, and rejects during development, allowing some possibilities to continue while others leave the current view. Broad reading and embodied perception supply new relations, so selection and generation can propel each other. Keeping many meaningful possible worlds available is compatible with this selection; the danger is fixing one view as the only answer before understanding consequential differences among the possibilities.

Sufficient exploration is relative to the question and its consequences: whether important alternatives that could change the judgment have become visible, which disagreements need further material, and when there is enough support to act. This gives exploration a direction and a stopping condition. Formalization contributes when relations need clarification, consequences need verification, or a work needs realization. An initial unfolding of relations gives it something meaningful to articulate and check. [Participation, access, and support](participation-and-support.md#why-organize-participation-this-way) explains how this arrangement serves the cultivation of intuition, the senses, and broad reading.

A relationship can become recognizable through an analogy, scene, image, or sense of mismatch before a complete sentence is available. Expression unfolds it through return, correction, and changes of vocabulary. A fragment can carry several developing relations, and different reconstructions can bring different parts into focus. Fidelity is assessed by returning to the object, context, and purpose that the expression is trying to articulate.

“Neural generation and symbolic constraint” names a functional analogy. Contextual generation can disclose possibilities, judge them, reject them, and revise them. Explicit representations make assumptions, provenance, priorities, conflicts, and proofs inspectable. Statistical assessment addresses uncertainty and fit; logical checking exposes invalid connections. These functions can overlap within either participant's contribution. The distinction helps organize their cooperation according to what a particular inquiry needs.

Logic contributes a callable checking capacity. Truth-seeking, corrigibility, relevant evidence, and avoiding arbitrary consequences guide developed factual arguments even when their representation changes. A chosen value orientation can supply a starting point for expression and action: a person may explore what follows from valuing autonomy or cultivation. Its consequences can be articulated before the orientation has been justified to every possible audience. Factual claims made along the way remain answerable to evidence, and inferential claims to their premises and connections. Checking begins with whether the reconstruction concerns the expressed object and purpose, then asks what kind of support its conclusion calls for.

## A working ontology forms with its question

A working ontology articulates the objects, relations, and constraints that matter for a question. Its concepts can be introduced, weakened, replaced, or reorganized. A useful distinction may initially be carried by context and become formal when it starts bearing inferential weight. More definitions can increase both expressive power and the cost of changing the account.

Economy asks added structure to contribute to explanation, prediction, or action. A “minimal sufficient model” expresses this preference for restrained complexity under the question's actual evidence. Sufficiency is assessed through the question it answers and the consequences it makes available; different models may serve different uses. A premise that simply restates the desired conclusion may make an inference valid while contributing little explanation. [Finite inquiry and abstraction](finite-inquiry-and-abstraction.md) distinguishes representation cost from metaphysical commitment and ultimate reduction.

Changing a question can change its ontology. Asking whether a program executed
an operation requires distinctions among calls, states, and outputs. Asking
whether it accomplished a purpose also requires the intended result and its
conditions of use. The first answer can be correct while leaving the second
question unanswered. A useful reformulation makes this added relation explicit
and checks which earlier conclusions still apply.

## Sources, candidates, and the active workspace

| Position | What it retains | What revision changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source | Utterances, documents, actions, measurements, and their provenance. | Appraisal and annotation can change without replacing the original occurrence with an interpretation. |
| Candidate | Competing explanations or models formed from sources. | Evidence can alter, compare, repair, or defeat them. |
| Active workspace | The sources, candidates, concepts, and checks used for the current question. | Attention can switch, prune, unload, or later recover them. |

Attention, retention, and acceptance have different effects. Removing a candidate from current use frees the workspace while leaving its source and possible later reconsideration available. Retaining incompatible explanations preserves alternatives for comparison; accepting their claims together as classical premises would expose the inference to contradiction. A source can be worth retaining while its explanation is still developing. The present question determines which branches are worth pursuing.

Before comparing conflicting candidates, establish whether their assertions
concern the same object, time, and conditions. Yesterday's failed run and
today's successful run can both be accurately reported. [The stream and time](flow-and-time.md)
develops how retained reports bear on a present judgment.

## Seeking explanations and comparing consequences

Abduction seeks possible explanatory structures from observations; deduction develops consequences within a proposed structure. A response, failure, or unexpected result can indicate how a system filters input, which distinctions it uses, or which assumption is missing. Several explanations may predict the same response. A single observation rarely identifies a unique internal mechanism, and an interaction can change the conditions it is probing.

Consider a fictional inquiry into a program that reports completion while an expected result is absent. One candidate says the result was written somewhere else; another says the action failed and the report was mistaken. Each organizes the same initial observation differently. Checking the relevant destination and the action's observable effects may support one candidate, defeat both, or disclose that “completion” meant a narrower operation. That last outcome changes the object being assessed: completion of a step and completion of the intended task have different consequences. The inquiry can therefore revise its question as well as its answer.

Candidate comparison needs both directions. First reconstruct possible assumptions and relations; then derive consequences that would distinguish them and compare those consequences with observation. An internally coherent story is insufficient. A prediction can fail because of an unsupported premise, an omitted condition, a mistaken reconstruction, or an unsuitable inference method. Locating the failure helps decide what to revise.

Comparing consequences also identifies where support ends. A conclusion
supported by every member of a nonempty admissible candidate family is robust
relative to that family; a shared omission can still defeat it in the world.
When candidates disagree, a further observation may distinguish them, or a
limited conclusion may survive the disagreement. [The preference harness](preference-harness.md)
develops the separate choices of which views to consider and what support
to require for acceptance.

The distinction matters because choosing what to investigate is not yet deciding what to believe. A candidate may deserve attention because its consequences are important or easy to test while remaining poorly supported. Conversely, a well-supported local conclusion can be accepted without exhausting every imaginable alternative. Answerability to evidence and finite completion belong together: the view states what it warrants under its conditions, while the stream remains open to later correction.

## Shared scientific inquiry

Analysis, explanatory hypothesis formation, synthesis, deduction, and empirical assessment can organize shared inquiry. Their relations allow return, overlap, and entry from an existing model. Peirce's discussion of inquiry provides a historical reference for distinguishing explanatory hypotheses, experiential consequences, and assessment: see [the 1908 manuscript, MS 841](https://fromthepage.com/jeffdown1/c-s-peirce-manuscripts/ms-841-1908-a-neglected-argument-text-and-fragments?page=9). These distinctions help ask where a contribution enters and what subsequent assessment it needs.

A human–model collaboration distributes work by contribution and capability. Intuitive generation and recognition can enter anywhere; the model can contribute candidates as well as articulate, compute, investigate, and check. A partial intuition can orient an investigation, and a model's formulation can make its implications visible. Factual support comes from the relevant observations and warranted connections developed through that work.

Learning and accomplishing a task remain distinguishable purposes. A collaboration can create opportunities to generate, recognize, and revise relationships while carrying explicit work through to its intended result. Domain methods determine which participation is useful. External records preserve relevant context for recovery and comparison. The actual allocation grows from the situated collaboration: what the person wants to develop, what each participant can contribute, and what the work needs.

## More than one layer of feedback

An observation, method, formal design, or particular claim can itself become the object of inquiry. A result at one level can prompt revision at another. Relations, constraints, priorities, and causal dependencies have different meanings; representing them as undifferentiated edges loses what inference needs. Speaking order is only one path through the activity.

A schematic notation gathers these distinctions:

$$
S_t=(D_t,z_t,C_t,\mathcal H_t),\qquad
W_t(q)=\operatorname{View}(S_t,q),\qquad
S_{t+1}=U(S_t,o_{t+1}).
$$

$D_t$ is available source-bearing material; $z_t$ denotes relations not yet fully propositionalized; $C_t$ denotes recognized tensions; $\mathcal H_t$ is a candidate family. $W_t(q)$ articulates relevant material and candidate ontologies, priorities, and inference policies for a current question. $U$ names changes occasioned by expression, perception, action, and external events. The notation gathers the functional relations discussed here; studying a particular realization means specifying how its material is retained, accessed, and transformed.

Prediction and world-directed feedback connect local views with later activity. New support or mismatch can change a candidate, the question, the ontology, or how models are generated and checked. A research cycle from definition to theorem to proof can be revisited through experiment and redefinition; that cycle has a different object from an acyclic priority relation among conflicting assertions. Feedback in the research does not violate acyclicity inside the formal problem.

Branches and snapshots offer computer-science images for retained alternatives and question-relative views. A branch keeps a candidate available for further development; a snapshot makes a local arrangement inspectable. Their usefulness comes from selective retention and recoverability. Useful stability and finite task completion coexist with continued revisability: a view can answer its present question while later activity brings new objects or evidence into consideration.

Reasoning reaches a local result when a conclusion is clear enough to guide
an act and expose its grounds. Its scope indicates what has been answered,
which alternatives remain consequential, and what new evidence would require
reconsideration. The activity can finish without becoming immune to correction.
This leaves a more precise question: how do we choose the views to work with,
and what evidence and inferential rules warrant accepting their conclusions?
The [preference harness](preference-harness.md) develops these distinct
contributions of selection and support.
