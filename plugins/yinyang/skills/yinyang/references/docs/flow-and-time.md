# Forming a life trajectory

[Home](../../README.md) · [简体中文](flow-and-time.zh-CN.md)

*The flame keeps its outline by changing. The stream carries what has happened without holding every drop in place. A view is drawn beside the current; by the time we return to it, the drawing has joined the river's history.*

As person and model read, make works, solve problems, and maintain an environment together, their activity leaves traces. Here, a **life trajectory** names those traces insofar as they can be revisited, connected, and explored further: what was said, chosen, attempted, made, encountered, and subsequently understood differently. It forms gradually through shared work and becomes living material the participants can carry into their next activity.

This essay explains how the trajectory forms and is retained. The following essays develop its intelligibility: how material opens questions and candidate explanations, how a chosen direction differs from grounds for accepting a conclusion, and how much must be retained to continue. By the third part, the participants have a trajectory they can explore together, reflecting on their contributions and cultivating their relationship and capabilities further.

## Shared work produces the trajectory

Imagine person and model improving a reading environment together. The person initially wants material to be easy to find and articles to be comfortable to read. The model organizes entrances and builds a page; both inspect it. Actual reading reveals that, although the material is available, frequent navigation breaks the connections between passages. They reconsider the problem: the environment needs to preserve both access to material and continuity within reading. The model changes layout and links, and the person judges their fit through further reading.

This work leaves a wish, an initial interpretation, versions of a work, actual operations, experience of use, and revisions. The trajectory connects them so later participants can ask why an arrangement arose, which use changed a judgment, what the current version preserves, and what remains unresolved. Cooperation produces the trajectory while accumulating an environment; a completed personal philosophy is not a prerequisite for beginning that work.

## Git/GitHub and Computer History

The trajectory brings histories from different places together so they can be retrieved, compared, and interpreted jointly. Two main entrances illustrate this gathering: **Git/GitHub version history** and **ChatGPT’s Computer History**. The former retains changes to works and configuration; the latter retains computer activity across applications within its enabled observation scope. It brings reading, browsing, making, conversation, and use of the environment into history, connecting changes to works with actual activity.

| Material | What it retains in the reading example | Its contribution to the trajectory |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Git/GitHub version history | Changes to pages, indexes, and configuration, with earlier versions available. | Compare differences in works and locate what a revision actually changed. |
| ChatGPT’s Computer History | What was encountered in applications and webpages, how works were read, switched between, operated, and used, and the relevant traces those activities left. | Place versions within actual use, connecting an episode of reading, conversation, or revision with surrounding activity. |

These two kinds of history are the main entrances for organizing material. Histories retained elsewhere can also join the same trajectory through time, objects, and provenance. In the reading example, version history lets the participants compare what changed in the page, while computer activity history lets them return to how it was read and used. Connecting the expression and feedback within those activities supports further understanding of why a revision was made and whether it helped. Records supply revisitable material; shared exploration develops an understanding of the experiences it concerns.

## From scattered records to revisitable relationships

A useful trajectory lets later participants find relevant material and understand its connections: which problem prompted a revision, which interpretation was adopted, which version an operation concerned, and what later feedback addressed. Provenance, time, objects, and links in existing records can carry these relations. In the reading example, finding the second page version should also let the participants return to the reading feedback that prompted it, rather than encounter only a line saying “improve the experience.”

Two related expressions develop here. Event sources retain what was said, done, or observed at the time; a current account distills purposes, judgments, usable arrangements, and unresolved questions now considered important. The current account helps work resume quickly, while sources let the participants examine and rewrite it. Concluding that the first version addressed the wrong problem need not erase that version: it explains how they reached the present.

The trajectory also contains branches. An unused layout can remain a candidate, and a failed attempt can show why a condition matters; details with no contribution to later understanding need not remain in working attention. Continuation and inquiry determine the scale of accumulation: adopted understanding remains usable, while important alternatives and corrections remain revisitable. The distinctions between streams, time, and memory below explain how such a trajectory can preserve change while supporting present action.

Git branches can give alternatives places to develop, while differences make retained changes comparable. A file can contain several candidates and a view can span several works, so branches do not map one-to-one to views. The commit graph records acyclic ancestry; a functional return from use to method and then to a later work produces new events within that history. [Recursive improvement](literary-recursive-improvement.md#gitgithub-making-change-comparable-and-continuable) connects those versions with the reasons and consequences that make the return intelligible.

## Sources for thinking in streams

Continuity is more than remaining unchanged. A process with memory carries earlier encounters into present activity and anticipates what has not yet happened. The histories of a person's activity and a model's interactions can be organized as event streams: sensing, interpreting, expressing, and acting leave occurrences connected through time and situation. Records preserve revisitable parts of these histories. From those histories and other relevant material, inquiry develops conceptual models of the participants; the person and model counterpart are the subjects being understood. [Heraclitus's measured fire (B30)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fragments_of_Heraclitus_%28annotated%29#Fragment_30) and [daily Sun (B6)](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fragments_of_Heraclitus_%28annotated%29#Fragment_6) gather change and persistence; [the stealing-fire essay](stealing-fire.md) develops their philosophical background.

William James's [*The Principles of Psychology*, chapter IX (1890)](https://www.yorku.ca/pclassic/James/Principles/prin9.htm) begins with thinking as ongoing activity and describes its personal, changing, continuous, and selective character. His stream of thought provides a psychological precedent for understanding continuity through changing relations rather than a chain of isolated contents. The computational event stream used here extends a functional vocabulary to model interaction.

Bergson's [*Time and Free Will*, chapter II](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/56852/pg56852-images.html) distinguishes lived duration, whose states interpenetrate, from homogeneous time represented through juxtaposed units. This complicates any easy equation between a lived stream and a sequence of recorded updates. Record order and timestamps organize selected observations for a question; they need not exhaust the continuity being modeled. The comparison preserves a productive tension between an inspectable representation and what its discrete arrangement may leave out.

These accounts supplement the Heraclitean foundation with psychological and temporal distinctions, making continuity, selection, and representation more intelligible. The following organization of records, present understanding, and actual change develops those questions within shared work.

## Retention and representational lag

Retained material affects how the participants understand the next encounter.
A current working view develops from that material, ongoing experience,
relevant knowledge, and shared purposes, stating what will guide present
judgment and action. Finding an earlier record provides an entrance for return;
understanding its meaning requires relating it to the current question, version,
and conditions. A new use can add a fact or change what the participants
understand by convenient reading or completed work.

Change takes place in several connected locations: material can be supplemented,
a description rewritten, an interface modified, and a reading habit gradually
altered. Interpretation and actual use connect these changes, each with its own
timing. Records make the connections available for later examination; participants
still judge what changed, why, and whether it improved the original activity.

An environment can change before an inquirer notices, while the inquirer's actions can change that environment. Memory therefore makes representational lag possible. An old observation can remain accurate; a recent observation can mislead. Applicability under current conditions is the question to check. Temporal age matters through the relevant changes: yesterday's measurement may still describe a stable object, while an observation from a moment ago may concern a condition that has already changed.

## External memory and event traces

The functional comparison human ≈ agent includes finite attention and working context. Person and model can both distribute retention into an environment. Files, notes, and knowledge bases let useful material remain available beyond what a participant currently holds. Search or retrieval-augmented generation can bring relevant portions back into a working context.

A maintained memory account articulates what presently seems useful: purpose, decisions, conditions, and unfinished questions. Attributable event sources retain evidence of earlier occurrences, including what was said, changed, or observed. These have different roles. Revising a summary need not erase its source, and retaining a source does not require continuing to accept its earlier interpretation. For example, a project can keep the original request, a version of its implementation, and later use feedback while updating its current explanation.

At suitable granularity, links from earlier evidence or action to later revisions or results can organize these traces as a directed acyclic provenance graph. The edges express recorded dependencies with a forward occurrence order; repeated feedback becomes successive events. This representation does not make every mental or social relation acyclic, establish causation merely from temporal order, or guarantee complete coverage. An environment-wide activity record and a project's version history supply differently scoped evidence. Their useful contribution is to recover relevant encounters and test an account against sources and current conditions, rather than make all of life continuously present.

In the reading environment above, the first page version, its later publication, and subsequent reading feedback can have different times. Connecting their records distinguishes a changed work, a changed environment, a changed experience, and a changed current interpretation. Those changes may coincide or be separated by an interval.

## Three meanings of now

| Object | What is current | What its actuality establishes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| External situation | Relevant environmental occurrences and conditions. | The condition a world-directed judgment aims to describe. |
| Lived experience | Present feeling, bodily response, or awareness. | That an experience occurs; its interpretation is another question. |
| Working model | The account currently used to understand or act. | What is represented or accepted within that account. |

Each object has its own conditions of change. Experience can persist while the associated external situation changes; an accepted judgment can change before the experience does. Explaining the connections among them means investigating how information was acquired, what sustains the response, and which new encounters alter it.

Consider a fictional traveler who learned yesterday that a bridge was closed. Today the bridge reopens. Before checking, the traveler still plans a detour; after seeing the official notice, they revise the plan, though a feeling of hesitation may remain. The bridge's condition, the current hesitation, and the accepted route description each have their own actuality. The revised plan responds to today's opening, yesterday's closure remains part of the history, and hesitation can continue into the changed situation.

An experience has an occurrence time; the event from which its content was learned has a source time; the represented condition has a reference time. A judgment also has its own time and conditions of validity. A prediction occurring now concerns a possible future rather than information already received from that future. Recollection is a current occurrence with past-directed content.

These times explain how the traveler can be mistaken about a present bridge
while accurately remembering a past notice. Correcting the route requires
updating the notice's present applicability, not declaring that the earlier
notice or the current hesitation never existed. Temporal distinctions locate
the correction rather than making every retained content equally current.

## Change and contradiction

“This bridge was closed yesterday” and “this bridge is open today” can both be
true. Two records saying that the bridge is open and closed today under the same
conditions present a conflict to investigate. Recording someone's claim that
the bridge is open also differs from accepting that it is open. Before invoking inconsistency tolerance, identify the referent, represented time, conditions, and level of each claim. An experience, a report of it, and a proposition about its external object are distinct.

Indexing clarifies content without dissolving every conflict. Claims incompatible under the same relevant conditions remain incompatible. Differently dated claims can also conflict when an accepted rule requires persistence across the interval. The content and constraints decide, not timestamps alone. [Repair semantics](preference-harness.md#selection-and-inference) handles inconsistent formalized data under an ontology; it is unnecessary merely because a process changes or a feeling differs from a judgment.

## Update order and elapsed time

First, second, and third versions order revisions; clock time lets us compare
how long passed between them. Bodily, linguistic, and environmental changes
can continue concurrently between records. An absence of new statements does
not mean activity has stopped. An interface may change on one day while a new
reading habit becomes stable through later repeated use. Version order and the
pace of actual development need separate examination.

Two forms of temporal separation matter here. **Representational separation**
lets a current recollection concern the past or a prediction concern the future.
**Different update rhythms** let an environment, a person's response, and an
accepted description change at different rates. The first concerns what a
representation is about; the second concerns when processes change. Either
can be useful, and neither alone establishes that a judgment is outdated.

## Retention with correction

Correction asks which retained relations remain useful, which need narrower scope, and which present commitments should be withdrawn. Withdrawing a judgment changes what is accepted now while preserving the earlier event and any continuing experience as distinct objects. The external judgment is assessed through evidence about its referent and time; the continuing experience is understood through its own occurrence and relations.

Perception, action, discussion, and checks can alter which retained account
applies now. Keeping provenance allows the inquirer to explain that change:
which earlier observation supported the old judgment, what new occurrence
challenged it, and which continuing condition still matters. External records
and shared inquiry make these relations recoverable without requiring the
whole history to remain in working attention.

The traveler can now cross the bridge and complete the journey. The route description serves the present action, while the retained history helps explain how the plan changed. A view serves a stretch of the stream; using it, revising it, and finishing the activity all become further events within that continuity.

Returning to the shared reading environment, the participants can now move from the current page back to earlier versions, operations, and feedback, understanding how the arrangement formed. Their next activity can retain a useful layout or investigate a reading interruption still unexplained. The trajectory supplies material; [Reasoning within an unfinished stream](reasoning.md) next develops how questions, compared explanations, and further judgments and actions emerge from that material and continue the trajectory.
