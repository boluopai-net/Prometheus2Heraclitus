# Teaching from a course's actual materials

Use this branch when a course combines sources or its usable study entrance is
unclear. Books, papers, notes, slides, recordings, transcripts, notebooks,
assignments, labs and projects can contribute to the same course. Their formats
do not define separate learning tracks or a required bundle. The learning path
chooses direction and scope; this method makes the selected course teachable.
The root owns teaching and learning evidence; [materials](course-materials.md)
owns saved companions, and [workspace](course-workspace.md) resolves their owners.

## Select sources by their contribution

Recover the selected scope, prior exposure and next unresolved relation from
the path, actual sources and relevant State. Inspect the course structure and
representative content before choosing a spine. A file count, extension,
prestigious course name or syllabus topic does not establish that its content
supplies the needed explanation.

Choose a primary source or coherent combination that develops the current
objects and relationships. Explain what it teaches, why it matters here and
which units to use. Complement it where another source contributes a missing
premise, figure, example, empirical comparison or computational behavior.
Reuse prior coverage and identify the new contribution rather than assigning
duplicates. Keep peripheral detail, repeated presentations and later extensions
reachable as references outside the active selection. These roles may change
across units; they are not permanent media rankings or required metadata labels.

Judge importance through the learning purpose, conceptual dependencies,
consequences of misunderstanding, source quality and distinct contribution.
Preserve the theory needed to understand the object while reducing repetition
and mechanical load. A textbook can be the spine for one unit and a notebook's
model and explanation for another. The official course order can reveal
dependencies without making its whole assessed workload compulsory. Keep
selection and omission reasons intelligible; wider curriculum changes remain
with the path owner.

## Combine formats by what they actually contain

| Material | Contribution to inspect | Teacher's work |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Books, papers and substantial notes | Connected definitions, arguments, evidence, examples or derivations | Select actual chapters/sections and preserve their edition, assumptions and role. Separately selected book study retains its own material contract. |
| Slides or PPT | Structure, figures, concise claims and prompts | Recover omitted reasoning and context from compatible sources or explicit teacher synthesis; develop an explanation rather than paraphrase bullets. |
| Video/audio and transcripts/captions | Spoken explanation, cases, demonstrations and classroom context | Match the actual lecture/segment and resolve references to unseen figures or gestures. A transcript does not contain every visual; slides do not reproduce the spoken argument. |
| Notebooks, code and recorded outputs | Explanation, computation, visualization and interpretation | Connect inputs, operations, outputs and meaning. Inspect/run the needed slice when a behavioral claim depends on it; distinguish stored output from a current verified run. |
| Assignments, discussions, solutions and exams | Questions exposing distinctions, assumptions or methods | Select/adapt useful problems with checked answers or reasoned evaluation grounds; preserve source versus teacher authorship and the chosen study mode. |
| Labs and projects | An extended question, implementation, observations and consequences | Identify the useful conceptual/empirical contribution and actual workspace; distinguish model execution, learner judgments and claimed replication. |

Inspect what a resource actually is. A short label such as record may denote
a recording, recap or activity log; it does not settle the resource's role.
Check compatibility of course years and versions. When slides, text and outputs
disagree, resolve the affected claim rather than silently choosing one.

A missing format is harmless when existing sources adequately support the
unit. A substantive gap calls for its corresponding repair: retrieve a needed
source, supply a grounded explanatory bridge or diagram, prepare a useful
teacher-authored question, or explicitly narrow supported coverage. Identify
which explanation or operation remains unsupported when access is insufficient.
Do not invent an instructor's argument, official answer, unseen visual,
empirical result or successful run. Empty sections and unnecessary activities
do not repair absent media categories.

Honor the selected reading/listening/viewing mode. A text-based route can use
notes and reliable transcripts with figures and teacher explanation without
assigning recordings. If an essential demonstration cannot be recovered in that
mode, name that particular gap and continue supported units. Preserve original
sources; the guide is an attributed explanation and synthesis, not a mislabeled
official transcript.

## Prepare a reachable learning entrance

Use the existing main guide as the ordinary course entrance. Its opening makes
the selected scope and an actual starting unit clear, linking directly to that
explanation and its needed source passage. On return, recover the current
position from State and open the corresponding unit; a static first-unit
recommendation does not reset progress. Keep the material index as the source
inventory rather than requiring the learner to assemble a lesson from it.

Develop connected explanations, examples and fitting visuals in the chosen
knowledge order. Associate each unit with the source pieces it uses, nearby
glossary entries and applicable training. Make the next connection or return
point apparent. Filename lists, unexplained slide summaries and promises to
write later are not prepared teaching. Source links support checking and depth;
they do not replace central explanation with repeated navigation.

Retain one independent glossary for recognition and recovery, and applicable
training under the existing materials contract and its exceptions. Reuse prior
companions at their owner. Incidental reference books do not each require a new
complete companion set; explicitly requested book sets retain their agreed
scope. Slides, transcripts and notebooks of one unit do not need separate
companion bundles. Split a long guide into unit files only when actual size or
use warrants it, keeping one entrance and reciprocal navigation.

Before handoff, follow the path entry to the guide, starting/resumption unit,
source, glossary and selected training. Confirm that a reader can tell what
to use first, what to understand and where to continue without reconstructing
the folders. Name real source/preparation gaps beside affected units.
Prepared files and model demonstrations do not advance learning State.
