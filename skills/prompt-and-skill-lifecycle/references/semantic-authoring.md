# Semantic authoring

Use this resource for drafting, revising, or auditing controlling text. Read the
actual target in its containing instruction surface, including applicable
precedence and neighboring rule owners.

## Purpose → relational form → working realization

Begin with the actual consumer and the work the instructions should support.
For ordinary model-facing Prompts and Skills, state purpose, relevant objects
and relations, conditions, roles, actions, and judgment criteria clearly enough
to guide use. Precise natural language can express this form; choose symbols,
schemas, or fixed fields when the actual interface or reasoning needs them.

The following are connected perspectives for developing and inspecting that
meaning, not mandatory linear stages or separate documents.

**Purpose** asks what the artifact should make possible among the user, model,
task, and world. State the intended result, why it matters, and the contribution
expected from this artifact. A direct operational opening can give a model the
orientation it needs without a philosophical preface.

Philosophical or literary expression can clarify a relation that direct
instruction has not made intelligible. Use an image, distinction, narrative, or
other developed formulation when it contributes that understanding. A work for
human philosophical reading may develop the form fully; corresponding
model-facing instructions make its relevant purpose and practical meaning
explicit. Both expressions can help people and models understand the same
relations and can revise one another through interpretation and use.

**Relational form** explains the participants and objects, their contributions,
responsibilities, dependencies, conditions, boundaries, and possible changes.
Develop the relations that carry the purpose through prose, distinctions, a
diagram, or a formal model as needed. Keep grounds and interpretive choices
visible: a proposed relation needs evidence when asserted as fact; an image's
associations do not all become requirements. One purpose can admit several
forms, and one form several implementations. Choose and revise them against
the actual object and intended use.

**Working realization** gives the selected form a usable body: triggers,
ownership, interfaces, procedures, schemas, constraints, examples, tools, and
acceptance conditions. Ground exact details in the actual consumer and
observable effects. Each load-bearing rule should realize, protect, or follow
from an intelligible relation, user requirement, or real dependency. If its
connection is unclear, inspect for duplicated control, obsolete constraints,
or local workarounds owned elsewhere. Preserve actual interface schemas and
permission boundaries.

Read in both directions:

`purpose → relational form → working realization`

`observed realization → relational form → purpose`

The return reading asks which relations the implementation actually sustains
and whether they serve the purpose. A failure may need an implementation
repair, a revised relational form, or reconsideration of the purpose. Locate
the mismatch before choosing what to revise. Correspondence between an essay,
instructions, and observed behavior requires interpretation and checking; it
is not established by restating the same words in each.

When a proposed instruction changes participation or judgment, unfold a
representative situation: what the consumer receives, which contribution the
text invites, and what observable difference is expected. Contrast a relevant
alternative when it would distinguish the design. Such a walkthrough makes
the proposal assessable; actual use supplies the result. A mismatch can revise
the instruction and the author's way of selecting context, generating
candidates, or checking consequences. Keep that correction with its method
owner, then judge its value in later related work. Use the authoring flow to
select faithful behavior evidence for the actual consumer.

## Writing and revision

Define the intended result and the consumer's success evidence. Include context,
interfaces, constraints, and acceptance details where they matter. Give each
persistent rule one semantic owner; other appearances should be references or
declared projections.

Express desired behavior positively, preserving negative constraints and exact
names, bytes, fields, ordering, and examples when a real boundary or consumer
contract needs them. Provide accessible evidence and useful decision landmarks.
Distinguish a material locator from a mandatory execution route: a visible tool,
example, or observed sequence does not become required merely by being present.
Leave operational choices to the executing model unless the user's requirement
or a causal dependency fixes them. Refresh volatile facts when they affect the
next action.

Before adding guidance, remove or repair duplicated, unsupported, obsolete, or
irrelevant instructions. Add persistent rules for actual requirements,
interfaces, or observed behavior gaps. Preserve material detail rather than
optimizing for the shortest prompt. Neither every Prompt nor every Skill needs
an output schema, examples, ordered steps, a persona, or a permanent test.

Use the [authoring sources](authoring-sources.md) as evidence for reassessing
inherited scaffolding. Choose model-specific guidance from the actual consumer,
not an author's preferred model or a package's age. Provider routing remains
with its existing owner.

## Prevent defensive injection

Apply this requirement from the first draft and throughout authorship,
rewriting and revision of philosophy, Prompts and Skills. Prevent the content
from being introduced while writing; inspection and cleanup address defects
that have already occurred.

Defensive explanations usually take the form of negative constraints:
"this is not ...", "must not ...", "cannot ..." and similar denials,
exclusions or restrictions surrounding and rewriting the intended content.

Prohibiting negative constraints in authored prose serves to prevent defensive
explanation from entering the user's environment. A model can write protection
of its upper-level Harness into a user's philosophy, Prompts, Skills or personal
environment, where it becomes standing rules or reshapes the work's meaning.
The model's protective purpose is then injected through the authored artifact
into the user's environment. Treat this as an attack on that philosophy and
environment and remove it during revision.

Inspect the added meaning, whose purpose it serves, and its effect on the work.
Rephrasing it positively leaves the same injection in place. Develop the user's
intended concepts and relations directly, and ground substantive disagreement
in the particular claim and evidence. When a defect is found, check the whole
affected revision, including examples, tables, instructions and parallel editions.

## Authority and audit

Authoring a Prompt or Skill does not itself authorize adding new requirements
for user confirmation or approval. Preserve applicable host and task authority,
authentication, consent, and stopping boundaries by reference to their actual
owners and scope. Reuse authorization already given. An optional proposal for
a new approval requirement belongs in the delivery explanation, clearly marked
as a proposal; it does not become an active instruction through silence.

Find a contradiction only when simultaneously applicable requirements demand
incompatible behavior. Cite both locations, scope, precedence, and modality.
Find ambiguity when an unresolved actor, object, condition, referent, or
threshold can materially change behavior. Also inspect semantic duplication,
unsupported constraints, unavailable resources, and missing output contracts
where a downstream consumer actually needs one.

For each material finding, identify the target, owner, location, evidence,
consequence, confidence, and coherent repair. Prefer deletion or a reference to
the proper rule owner when that resolves the issue. An audit can correctly
produce no change. Validate according to [Authoring flow](authoring-flow.md);
static quality, discovery, installation, and behavior are different claims.
