# Personal-First Humanism

[Home](../../README.md) · [简体中文](personal-first-humanism.zh-CN.md)

*A person encounters an arrangement made for people in general. Something
important in their life has no place in it. Making that difference intelligible
can begin the work of changing the arrangement.*

A philosophy for personal practice begins somewhere concrete: someone
perceives, understands, desires, makes, and lives through the consequences.
Categories such as user, worker, reader, and citizen make some relations
available. The person also brings circumstances and possibilities that those
categories may leave unexpressed. **Personal-first humanism** names an ethical
orientation for developing arrangements from that concrete encounter.

Its practical premise is explicit: people's developing understanding, voluntary
agency, and ability to sustain worthwhile lives should give tools and cooperative
arrangements their direction. A personal philosophy can help articulate what
that premise means here, while a model helps give it working form. Actual
consequences can then revise the arrangement and its account of the person.

## Real persons, revisable descriptions

Taking the person seriously does not require an exhaustive metaphysical theory
of personhood. It requires acknowledging the actual life to which a description
answers: embodied conditions, experiences, relations, commitments, and effects
that continue beyond the description. A model of someone can be inadequate
even when it is internally coherent. Their correction can disclose that
inadequacy; subsequent experience can disclose another.

The framework adopts **modest naturalism**: persons, psychological activity, machines, and
engineering occur in the same natural world. Within computational ontology,
**fire monism** organizes representations of becoming and transformation; fire
can represent a changing person without making that person part of fire. Process ontology asks how
things form, persist, and change. A domain ontology expresses that understanding
for a particular question through events, states, objects, relations, and
transitions, making human activity and change available for description,
reasoning, and use. A computational ontology explicitly expresses these
distinctions for computational use.
This naturalist orientation places the activity in a shared world; computational representation
articulates what we select and how we understand it. [Natural world and computational abstraction](world-and-expression.md#physical-ground-and-computational-abstraction)
develops this connection.

“The person is real” therefore has a practical force here. A category, metric,
or story does not exhaust the one who lives through its application. Its
consequences belong in the assessment. The same force applies to other people;
the encounter has more than one center of concern.

## Construction, nominalism, and fiction

The possibility of constructing a concept invites questions about what it
represents and how it is used. A nominalist question concerns the status of
universals and general categories. Hobbes's *Leviathan*, chapter IV, supplies
one historical formulation by treating universal names as applying to
individual things [[1]](#source-1). Such a formulation does not make those particulars
disappear.

Fictionalism raises another question: could a discourse be useful without
accepting its apparent claims as literally true? A selected fictional role
can organize conduct; a thought experiment can develop consequences under
imagined conditions. Computational ontology can represent these contents:
a god someone believes in, an imagined companion, or an invented cartoon
character can enter a process model through beliefs, expectations, narratives,
and actions. The imagining activity and its effects actually occur; the
character acquires its meaning within the imagining or story. The model can
therefore express who imagines what, how they use it, and what happens later.
The [metaphor essay](metaphor-and-self-revision.md) develops how expressive
constructions become interpretations and realizations.

These distinctions leave room to consider the stronger conditional: **if many
of the concepts through which we organize life are constructed or treated as
fictions, which constructions should we choose?** That question makes their
adequacy important. It does not establish that everything is fiction. Nor does
it derive an ethical answer from an ontological label. A construction could
serve administration, efficiency, conformity, discovery, or an individual's
development. Choosing among these directions requires reasons about what
should matter.

The personal-first premise supplies such a reason: concepts and arrangements
should support actual persons' understanding and agency, and remain open to
their corrections. Constructibility gives revision room; the ethical premise
gives revision a direction. The two contributions should remain distinguishable.

## Six connected dimensions

A philosophical account develops questions about the world, existence, truth,
knowing, value, and method. An adopted position gives a particular answer within
that inquiry. The distinction concerns the work a passage does: a theory unfolds
an explanation and its reasons; a position states the understanding adopted in
this practice. Both can be developed, criticized, and revised. “Worldview” remains
the familiar name for the overall picture in which these answers belong.

| Philosophical dimension and shared practice | Position or activity developed here | Contribution to modeling and practice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worldview | Modest naturalism places activity in one changing natural world. | Place persons, models, and their activities in a shared picture of the world. |
| Ontology | Process ontology examines how things form, persist, and change within the natural world. | Articulate the objects, events, relations, and continuity relevant to a domain model. |
| Theory of truth | Correspondence for claims about actuality; truth under an interpretation for statements in a model. | Specify what a claim says, its conditions of truth, and how an actual-world claim relates to its object. |
| Epistemology | Embodied, fallible inquiry that develops through evidence and correction. | Organize observations, candidate explanations, inferences, and tests. |
| Axiology | Personal-first humanism: concrete persons' lives, agency, and chosen development give arrangements their direction. | Form goals and evaluation criteria whose consequences return to the people affected. |
| Methodology | Question-relative pluralism. | Select and combine inquiry, dialogue, formalization, simulation, experiment, and practical construction. |
| Shared practice | Person and model understand, model, express, construct, and evaluate together in reality. | The six dimensions meet in the work; experience and consequences revise understanding and arrangements. |

Here **humanism** locates value in human lives and agency. **Personal-first**
brings that concern to the concrete person encountered, including circumstances
that a generic category can miss. “Personal-first humanism” names the developed
value position; “personal-first” and “personal-first orientation” emphasize its
practical priority. Other persons remain bearers of purposes and consequences
within the same concern. This is the connection between the essay's title, its
value argument, and the goal-setting that follows.

An engineering criterion expresses this position in a particular task. For a
reading interface, the goal might be to let someone follow a thought across
related passages with less navigation effort. That criterion is a selected
operational expression of the value position. The reader's experience can reveal
whether the arrangement supports understanding, and can lead both participants
to revise the criterion or the account that suggested it.

## Objects, models, expression, and realization

The six dimensions jointly guide domain understanding, purposes, and inquiry; the domain supplies
objects and events that the account seeks to understand. Between them, people
and models develop a conceptualization: a question-relative selection of
entities, distinctions, and relations. Engineering makes selected meanings
explicit and gives them usable forms.

In knowledge engineering, Gruber describes an ontology as an explicit
specification of a conceptualization; Guarino further distinguishes that
conceptualization from a theory expressing a vocabulary's intended meaning.
These accounts help locate a **domain ontology** among our representations of a
domain. Here a process-oriented domain ontology organizes participants, events,
states, relations, constraints, and changes. A conceptual model develops a
particular account with those distinctions. Clear natural language, diagrams,
or formal expressions can give that account a usable expression. Computational
ontology makes its conceptual relations explicit for a model to interpret and
use. In a [private sinthome](writing-a-sinthome.md#computational-ontology-in-a-private-sinthome),
relations among person, counterpart, work, correction, and continuation can
acquire this computational use through natural language.
[Gruber, 1993](https://tomgruber.org/writing/ontolingua-kaj-1993.pdf);
[Guarino, 1998](https://www.loa.istc.cnr.it/wp-content/uploads/2020/03/FOIS98.pdf).

| Role in the practice | What it contains | Example: returning to unfinished reading |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual world and shared activity | Persons, machines, events, material conditions, and consequences. | A reader loses the connection between two passages and tries to recover it. |
| Domain understanding and conceptual models | Selected objects and relations, proposed explanations, and relevant constraints. | Distinguish the reader, the passages, their conceptual connection, and the interruption; propose why navigation broke the connection. |
| Representation and expression | Language, diagrams, formal statements, data, and records carrying selected meaning. | Write the explanation, draw the reading path, or record where the reader stopped. |
| Engineering realization and use | Programs, tools, interfaces, and working arrangements that interpret and use representations. | Keep related passages together and make the reading position recoverable. |
| Philosophical framework | Developed explanations and adopted positions concerning world, existence, truth, knowing, value, and method. | Ask why continuity of understanding matters, what the explanation commits to, and what would warrant revising it. |

The roles distinguish relations within one practice. A document can carry a
conceptual model; a program can embody a decision about which objects matter;
a philosophical passage can itself be represented and examined. Their physical
carriers and the activities of making and using them belong to the actual world.
A representation reaches its subject through meaning and interpretation, while
its carrier exists and operates through material processes. These two relations
answer different questions.

The represented domain may concern actual life, a hypothesis, or a fictional
situation. Actual-world claims invite comparison with their objects; hypothetical
and fictional accounts develop consequences under their stated conditions.
A person's imagining is actual activity whose content can concern an imagined
character. Likewise, a participant, their activity history, selected records,
and a conceptual model of them occupy different roles in understanding that life.

[Six philosophical dimensions and shared practice](../diagrams/layers.html)
expands all six dimensions from the first table, stating each adopted position
and its contribution. Within practice, experience and questions inform domain
understanding; computational ontology and conceptual models acquire expression;
engineering brings them into use. Consequences can revise the understanding,
arrangement, and philosophical grounds.

Truth has a definite object here. Claims about actual life are assessed for
correspondence with facts. Statements within a model are assessed under its
interpretation and given conditions. Inferences from premises to conclusions
are assessed under the adopted rules of reasoning. Applying a model to reality
also requires examining its assumptions and their relation to the object.
People and language models can participate in all these judgments. The theory
of truth articulates what it means for a claim to hold; epistemology examines
the grounds for accepting, doubting, or revising the judgment.

Evidence representations therefore remain answerable to experience, evaluation
criteria to lived purposes, and executed procedures to judgments of methodological
fit. A well-formed representation can explain the phenomenon poorly; a predictive
model can serve a purpose we have reason to reject. Explanatory adequacy,
worthwhile purposes, and methodological fit jointly affect how we use and revise
models within the same practice.

“Begin with psychological adequacy before formal refinement” expresses a useful
research direction when the question concerns a psychological phenomenon:
recognize what needs explanation, then let formal tools clarify and test the
candidate structure. The activities can interleave. Adequacy means addressing
the phenomenon, including differences that an interpretation fails to explain.
Mathematical validity and empirical adequacy retain their distinct checks.

Preferences can choose a question, direct attention, or guide the adoption of
a working view. Evidence constrains what can be accepted as true about its
object. Intuition and abduction can propose a candidate before all its conditions
are explicit; comparison and feedback can develop or defeat it. This is why
the epistemological name above emphasizes a starting point and corrigibility.

## Adapt arrangements around people

A personal-first design asks how a difficulty is produced before asking the
person to absorb it. Some difficulties can be changed through a cooperative
arrangement: when work happens, how decisions are shared, or what others need
to receive. Others call for different software or an interface. A person can
also choose to learn, practice, or change a habit for an end they recognize.

Considering social arrangements and software before demanding personal
adaptation is a productive direction. It opens alternatives that a fixed
environment might conceal. Actual dependencies can require another order;
the question is which change serves the purpose under the circumstances.

For instance, an explanatory assistant might send every investigative detail
to the reader. Instead of treating the reader's overload as a defect to train
away, the collaborators can redesign the explanation around the judgment the
reader needs to make. The model can carry retrieval and implementation while
making reasons, alternatives, and material uncertainty intelligible. The
person participates through recognition, questioning, and decisions that matter.
If the purpose is learning the investigative method, the allocation can change.

The improvement should be assessed through actual work: can the person
understand the choice, influence its direction, and use what is produced?
Output volume and the model's reassurance are different observations.

## Society is an encounter among persons

Society includes institutions and norms that shape what people can do. It also
includes other persons with their own purposes, needs, and contributions.
A philosophy more adequate to one individual can improve shared arrangements
by revealing an overlooked possibility. It can also impose costs on others.
Those effects belong to its assessment.

Mill's *On Liberty*, chapter III, argues for individuality and different
experiments of living while retaining the condition of not injuring others [[2]](#source-2).
This offers a source for the connection between singular development and
plural coexistence. The present proposal develops it through revisable tools
and model-supported practices.

A common framework can enable people to cooperate without demanding identical
lives. Standards, stable interfaces, and shared procedures can protect useful
continuity; they should also be open to criticism where their effects defeat
their purposes. A person's disagreement is an occasion to investigate the
relation, not automatic evidence that every social constraint is harmful.

Models contribute to that inquiry as interpreting and generating counterparts.
Their participation can enlarge human agency while retaining independent
judgment. Personal-first humanism chooses a purpose for the arrangement;
it does not require the person to author every idea or perform every step.
The composition in [Sun, Moon, and Earth](yinyang-and-taiji.md#sun-moon-and-earth) keeps these contributions
and the independent world together.

## A changing person in a changing world

A person's understanding develops while tools and social conditions can also
change. A representation adequate to an earlier encounter may need revision
because the question, capabilities, or conditions have changed. Preserving
continuity means retaining what still serves the practice while making such
differences recognizable.

Carnap's account of framework adoption attends to purpose, fruitfulness, and
the effort involved in using a linguistic form [[3]](#source-3). It provides a comparison
for this choice among representations. [Embodied Experimental Philosophy](embodied-experimental-philosophy.md)
connects their selection with participation and later evidence. A system can
support reasoning; reflection can also examine its boundary and aim. Dynamic
representation and model selection are resources in this work.

Return to the person whose life had no place in a general arrangement.
Success need not mean that the person has learned to fit the old category.
An explanation may become intelligible, a tool usable, or a different way
of cooperating sustainable. The person may also choose a demanding change
in themselves. Humanism gives these transformations their measure: what
understanding, voluntary agency, and worthwhile activity do they make
possible, and what do they ask of other lives?

## Sources and scope

The connected naturalist and fire-monist account, the six dimensions, and their
model-supported realization state the philosophy adopted here. The sources
contribute the distinctions identified above; personal-first humanism gives
practice its value direction, developed and revised through actual consequences.

1. <a id="source-1"></a> Thomas Hobbes (1651), **Leviathan**, chapter IV, “Of Speech.” [Original text](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Leviathan_%281651%29/Chapter_4). One historical account of universal names and particular things, not a proof of the present ethical orientation.
2. <a id="source-2"></a> John Stuart Mill (1859), **On Liberty**, chapter III. [Original text](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/34901/34901-h/34901-h.htm). Individuality and experiments of living within plural coexistence.
3. <a id="source-3"></a> Rudolf Carnap (1950), **Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology**. [Public reprint](https://ditext.com/carnap/carnap.html). Purpose-sensitive adoption of linguistic frameworks; the personal-first premise is supplied separately here.
