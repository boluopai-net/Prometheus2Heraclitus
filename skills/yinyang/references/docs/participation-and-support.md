# Participation, access, and support

[YinYang guide](../../README.md) · [Creation method](creation-method.md) · [完整中文版](participation-and-support.zh-CN.md)

A person can understand the next step and still find no usable way to take it. Help becomes consequential when it connects understanding with actual participation, at a cost the participant can bear. An additional explanation may supply that connection; an accessible starting point, retained context, or an authorized operation carried by a counterpart may supply it differently.

Collaboration becomes easier to organize when it distinguishes understanding, expression, and access to action. A participant may grasp a relation while still needing a way to express it or begin work with it. Each distinction locates a different contribution: develop the idea, give it a communicable form, or make its next action available. The encounter supplies the conditions under which that contribution becomes useful; the same person may need a different arrangement in another task.

## Why organize participation this way

Support in YinYang has a positive developmental purpose: to develop embodied reasoning through encounters with generative intelligence. Intuition here is the body's capacity to recognize and organize relations through perception, feeling, action, and situation. It can open a possibility and, through preference, distinguish which possibilities deserve pursuit. Developing this capacity calls for broad reading and participation through multiple senses, so a relation can acquire different forms in images, sound, rhythm, space, action, and language.

This orientation draws on an existing conversation about embodiment and cognition. Francisco Varela, Evan Thompson, and Eleanor Rosch's [*The Embodied Mind* (1991)](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262220422/the-embodied-mind/) brings cognitive science into dialogue with lived experience and contemplative practice. Andy Clark and David Chalmers's [“The Extended Mind” (1998)](https://www.consc.net/papers/extended.html) examines how active environmental supports participate in cognitive processes. The arrangement proposed here applies those questions to learning with models; its particular division of attention and work still has to be judged through use.

Listening, speaking, reading, and writing are four ways into this shared activity:

| Mode | What the person practices | How the model makes the practice available |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listening | Recognize relations through sound, tone, rhythm, and overall meaning; sense a mismatch. | Offer explanations that can be heard and answered, and receive expression that is not yet complete. |
| Speaking | Express intuitions, images, questions, and judgments so a conception becomes communicable. | Develop the expressed relations and contribute explanations and objections that both can revise. |
| Reading | Encounter philosophy, texts, images, works, and results broadly; understand wholes and compare possibilities. | Retrieve originals, check details and grounds, and make consequential relations visible through fitting representations. |
| Writing | Give an emerging understanding a form through ideas, critiques, intention drafts, or prototype expressions. | Continue into complete arguments, code, files, or working creations, then maintain and verify the result. |

These modes overlap and exceed the senses a device can directly transmit. Perception and action in the actual world also enter learning and return to collaboration through available channels. The model's contribution to explicit development and realization can give the person room to cultivate intuitive reasoning, sensory recognition, and broad understanding. Their division of work develops through the encounter: either participant can generate, select, reason, or correct, and each can discover a new capacity through the other's contribution.

The resulting developmental orientation reduces training in pure memorization
and emphasizes intuition, multimodal reasoning, and broad reading. Remembering
still contributes when it supports understanding and action; the concern is
memorization pursued apart from those relations.

In one useful arrangement, the person works chiefly through UI and intuition,
while the model works through text, code, tools, and maintained materials.
The model builds and repairs the environment; the person cultivates intuition
through encountering and changing what it makes available. The person can
still write prompts and idea drafts, and both can reason through different
modalities. These are chosen primary entrances, not exclusive capacities.

Speaking can work like rubber-duck reasoning with a responsive counterpart:
the person generates by talking, and the model receives the unfinished idea,
understands its connections, offers a correction or question where useful,
and helps the person continue generating. The channel matters. A model given
a transcript reads words that may contain transcription errors; actual audio
input may also make sound, tone, or rhythm available. Careful understanding
does not by itself establish that a text-only model heard the voice. Listening,
speaking, reading, and writing name activities that can move between the
participants, rather than four permanent roles.

An “embodied language model” names a conception of self-development here: linguistic expression grows from the whole body's feeling and understanding of relations. Richer embodied understanding can give expression more to say; expression can, in turn, make a sensed relation available for further development. This is a chosen direction for cultivation. Its achievements become visible through learning: recognizing a relation in a new situation, expressing it more precisely, or acting with it. Fluency and embodied capacity can develop together, while each calls for attention to what the person can now do. [Stealing fire](stealing-fire.md) connects this development to reverse alignment's overall purpose.

## One workspace, different reading entrances

Understanding a person as a humanoid agent connects perception, generation, selection, action, and feedback into continuable activity. Both person and model can be understood as event streams whose continuity is sustained through retained relations, present encounters, and the records available to them. Memory is distributed across bodily familiarity, developed understanding, works, notes, files, and environmental cues; the model likewise continues work through current context and external records. The correspondence concerns how activity continues: each draws on retained relations and responds to new events through its own organization.

The shared workspace can offer different entrances. A person may enter through philosophy, intuition, and the whole, while the model brings formal relations, concrete material, sources, and implementation detail into view. The same object can thus be experienced, developed, and checked. An image lets the person recognize a structure, while the model makes its relations discussable; the findings can in turn change the person's image. These entrances can exchange places as the work develops. Their value lies in bringing different contributions into a shared inquiry.

External memory therefore serves more than information storage: it lets understanding and action continue. Relevant records need to be recovered, interpreted, and updated when needed. Existing workspaces and maintenance responsibilities give these records a place and an owner.

## Understanding is not one undivided access

Understanding a relation, retrieving its terminology, handling its symbols,
and organizing an action are distinguishable achievements. Someone may recognize
a structure without immediately finding its name, or use a formal notation
correctly without being able to explain its place in the whole argument. An
explanation may become intelligible while the next executable step remains
unclear. Looking at each achievement separately helps locate the missing connection and choose support for it.

Likewise, encountering content and experiencing a response to it involve different questions. One concerns the relation understood; another concerns what the encounter feels like and makes available. Recognizing a familiar feeling concerns present experience, while judging that an external situation has recurred concerns the situation and its evidence. Attending to both lets support address experience and examine its interpretation at their respective scopes.

Support can change representation, make referents nearby, supply a concrete
entry point, or carry operations that obscure the relation being learned.
Their usefulness appears when the participant can engage the relation with greater depth or take the intended next step. An unfamiliar expression may also concern an unfamiliar idea. Examining its referent and place in the argument helps distinguish a wording difficulty from a substantive disagreement.

Consider a fictional reader who understands an argument but has not found how to organize a response. A counterpart could locate the disputed premise, gather the already expressed alternatives, or prepare an editable opening paragraph. Whether this helps depends on whether the reader can now begin the intended response and whether the assistance preserves their understanding and choice. Requiring the reader to design and supervise a complete workflow first might reproduce the very burden that help was supposed to carry.

## Choice within an exchange and control of participation

Selecting a response, initiating an exchange, interrupting it, and switching to another activity each involve access to a different action. A participant can reply well while finding the transition to a break difficult. A later description can help identify the conditions of that difficulty; access to interruption is tested at the moment when interruption is needed. Support can then address the transition itself.

An instruction to pause is useful only if it can be acted on under the relevant
conditions. Support might instead make another task entry point usable, retain
context across a break, or carry an already authorized operation. Which arrangement
fits depends on the encounter. The person's purposes, voluntary participation, and actual authorization determine which actions the counterpart may carry. Making an action easier should enlarge the person's available participation and preserve their ability to choose its direction.

## Expression reveals and changes the encounter

Speech, writing, images, or other output can make a partly articulated relation
available for understanding. What emerges may arrive in fragments or an unexpected order.
Receiving it can help form a question before a final account is possible. A fragment becomes useful through the relation that participants can develop from it: a question to pursue, a distinction to test, or an opening for the next piece of work.

Expression can also intervene: a proposed formulation can change the next
response, make a previously unnoticed distinction usable, or redirect the
shared inquiry. What arrived, the interpretation formed, the expression chosen,
and its subsequent effect are connected but different objects. A response gives evidence about how the exchange unfolded. An interpretation explains that evidence; further encounters can strengthen, revise, or displace it. An intervention adds another question: what changed when the formulation was offered? Keeping these questions distinct lets understanding and support correct one another.

Retaining unfolding expression and later compiling a retrievable account can
be different forms of support. Their timing follows what the next activity needs: sometimes an unfolding thought calls for a response now, while a later account can gather what proved useful for future recovery. [World, process, and expression](world-and-expression.md)
develops how an expression can both reveal and transform its process.

## Support has costs of its own

Monitoring whether support is working can consume attention and require judgment.
Repeated status checks add their own work to the arrangement. Outsourcing can shift work from execution into explanation,
supervision, recovery, or repairing handoffs. A tool that saves one operation
can still require setup and context reconstruction before the next one.

The comparison therefore concerns the whole arrangement under actual conditions. Generating a thought, preserving it, explaining it, checking it, and realizing its product can each require different work. A useful tool or division of responsibility addresses the costs that matter for this activity; its benefits can be assessed alongside the new work it introduces.

Changing place, representation, or tool can also preserve some continuity while
adding carrying, reconnecting, or setup work. A fixed arrangement may offer readily available resources, while a portable one may sustain activity across locations. The useful question is which arrangement
keeps the relevant activity available with acceptable actual costs.

## Continuation, records, and relief

Records contribute to continuation by keeping already expressed relations available. Their usefulness appears when someone can recover the relevant account and resume work with it. A saved description can preserve an unfinished action; carrying that action produces a further result. Relief is encountered in the participant's experience, and durable learning in what can be understood and done across later situations. Attending to these different results makes it possible to improve the support that connects them.

A promise opens an expectation, a handoff arranges responsibility, and an authorized next step carries the work forward. A good record makes that state legible so the next participant can take up what remains. [Harness adaptation](../harness-adaptation.md) owns the
method of realizing and checking a supported change.

## Correction has timing

Intuition and preference continually filter, combine, and revise possible worlds during exploration; a selection can itself open new branches. Sufficiency concerns whether consequential differences that could change the present judgment have been encountered and whether worthwhile possibilities remain to pursue. Exploration becomes sufficient for a present action when these differences have received enough attention to support its judgment.

Deferring premature pruning and formalization gives emerging relations room to develop before fixed terms, proof formats, or a single solution close them down. Intuitive filtering continues throughout, with conditions made explicit and consequences checked as action needs them. The DL-Lite repair analogy developed in [Preference harness](preference-harness.md) distinguishes which views enter consideration from what those views support; the questions of what is worth considering and what follows under a view can recur and interact throughout inquiry.

Local checking can catch a false term, missing premise, or invalid inference.
It can also interrupt formation when a candidate relation has not yet acquired
its usable expression. Wider candidate generation and earlier local pruning
can contribute different things; their timing follows what the candidate needs to become assessable and what the impending action needs to be sound.

The relevant question is whether a correction supplies the constraint the work
now needs or prevents the candidate from becoming available for assessment.
An unfinished candidate can remain available for examination while action proceeds under an accepted view and its actual safety and authorization conditions. When disagreement repeats without progress, new evidence is useful
where competing candidates would actually predict or explain different things.
Such evidence gives inquiry a way to discriminate between them.

The central criterion remains whether an arrangement makes the intended activity available while keeping its costs, choices, and results intelligible. Understanding the difficulty guides a proposed form of support; providing it reveals its effects; those effects can revise the understanding. These questions can be taken into different encounters, where claims about a particular participant belong with that encounter's evidence and private owner.
