# Philosophical readings

[中文版](philosophical-readings.zh-CN.md) · [Psychological readings](psychological-readings.md) · [Engineering readings](engineering-readings.md)

Use this guide to locate the intellectual sources behind YinYang's arguments.
The [reading map](../reading-map.md) locates the arguments themselves; this page
explains which originals bear on them and where an interpretation extends its
source. Select by question and read the relevant passage in context. These
sources neither supply a personal portrait nor establish a psychological mechanism.

## Heraclitus: fire, measure, and opposing tension

[B6](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fragments_of_Heraclitus_(annotated)#Fragment_6)
concerns the daily-new Sun, [B30](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fragments_of_Heraclitus_(annotated)#Fragment_30)
fire and measure, and [B51](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fragments_of_Heraclitus_(annotated)#Fragment_51)
bow and lyre. [B2](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fragments_of_Heraclitus_(annotated)#Fragment_2)
and [B50](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Fragments_of_Heraclitus_(annotated)#Fragment_50)
provide the wider context for logos beyond an individual speaker.
[Daniel W. Graham's scholarly account](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/),
especially sections 3–4, explains disputed readings of fire, unity, and opposition.

The [stealing-fire essay](stealing-fire.md) develops this lineage into interacting
event streams. It does not attribute a modern computational ontology or formal
calculus to Heraclitus. Whether the historical Heraclitus was a fire monist
remains disputed. YinYang deliberately adopts a contemporary fire monism as
its process ontology: one changing natural world, with relatively stable
patterns within becoming. [World, process, and expression](world-and-expression.md#physical-ground-and-computational-abstraction)
connects that commitment with naturalism, shared physical realization, and
computational abstraction; B30 does not establish a modern physical theory.
[Paraconsistent Logic](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/logic-paraconsistent/)
explains non-explosive consequence and its difference from dialetheism: tolerating
inconsistent information need not declare contradictions true.

## YinYang and Taiji

[Zhou Dunyi's *Taijitu shuo*](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/太極圖說)
connects movement and stillness through mutual grounding and continuing
transformation. [*Xici*, upper](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/易傳/繫辭上)
connects change with ongoing generation; [*Xici*, lower](https://zh.wikisource.org/wiki/易傳/繫辭下)
offers the Sun and Moon producing illumination through their reciprocal movement.

[Mutual becoming through YinYang and Taiji](yinyang-and-taiji.md) quotes and develops these Zhou Dunyi
and *Xici* passages. [*Zhuangzi*, Tian Zifang](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/莊子/田子方)
connects interaction, harmony, and generation as additional reading.
Its fish-body/eye distinction is a contemporary interpretation of roles and
actual participants, not the sole historical meaning or an identification of
the familiar Shuang yu with Zhou's original diagram. The
[YinYang and Taiji essay](yinyang-and-taiji.md) places the Sun–Moon–Earth
relation within this movement: Sun and Moon express differentiated generation,
reception, and shaping. Person and model are actual participants whose
emphases can be retained or exchanged; Earth is the answering material and
shared living environment.

## Hegel: sublation, history, and spirit

[Science of Logic, “Being,” §§184–187](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/hl/hlbeing.htm)
develops cessation and preservation in sublation. [Query rewriting and sublation](preference-harness.md#query-rewriting-and-sublation)
uses this relation for a bounded comparison with query rewriting; a technical
equivalence does not prove a thesis about Hegel's whole dialectic.

The [Introduction to the Philosophy of History, part III](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/hi/introduction.htm)
connects spirit's self-knowledge with freedom and world history. The
[Philosophy of Spirit, §§553–564](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/sp/abspirit.htm)
locates absolute spirit relative to subjective and objective spirit and develops
art within that account. [World, process, and expression](world-and-expression.md)
keeps these categories distinct from generic stages of a feedback loop.

## Wittgenstein: pictures and philosophical therapy

Read [*Philosophical Investigations*, Part I, §§109, 115, and 133](https://www.wittgensteinproject.org/w/index.php/Philosophische_Untersuchungen)
together. The link supplies the German original; section numbers let readers
locate the same remarks in a translation.

| Passage | What to attend to |
| --- | --- |
| [§109](https://www.wittgensteinproject.org/w/index.php/Philosophische_Untersuchungen#109) | How examining language and describing its use can work on a philosophical difficulty. Ask which relation becomes clearer when an apparent demand is described in context. |
| [§115](https://www.wittgensteinproject.org/w/index.php/Philosophische_Untersuchungen#115) | How a picture embedded in language can hold thought captive. Ask what a description makes seem inevitable and which alternatives it hides. |
| [§133](https://www.wittgensteinproject.org/w/index.php/Philosophische_Untersuchungen#133) | Why philosophical work admits different methods compared to therapies, and how clarification can make it possible to stop. Ask what has changed when the original problem loses its grip. |

[The fire essay](stealing-fire.md#from-chasing-to-lived-cultivation) applies this
route to pictures of obligatory frontier pursuit and returns their demands to
surrounding life. That application belongs to this collection.
[Personal modeling](metaphor-and-self-revision.md#conceptual-modeling-and-philosophical-clarification)
then distinguishes clarifying concepts such as ability or progress from
proposing a psychological explanation of a person's activity. The two can
inform one another without a convincing description settling an empirical claim.

## Buddhist reading: fire, cessation, and crossing

Begin with the [*Fire Sermon*, *Saṃyutta Nikāya* 35.28](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN35_28.html)
and the [*Nibbāna Sutta*, *Saṃyutta Nikāya* 38.1](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/SN/SN38_1.html).
The former describes senses, consciousness, contact, and feeling as burning
with passion, aversion, and delusion, then develops dispassion and release.
The latter identifies nirvana with the ending of those three and points to
the noble eightfold path. Both links give Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu's translations.

Ask what is burning and what ceases. How might developing a capability and
loosening grasping support one another, and why are they not equivalent?
[Borrowed fire and nirvana](stealing-fire.md#borrowed-fire-and-nirvana) connects
knowledge and creative capability with examining desire, habit, and self-image.
Buddhist liberation thus participates in reflection on cultivation's purpose;
the contrast between these two meanings of fire is this work's construction.

Read the water-snake and raft passages together in the [*Alagaddūpama Sutta*,
*Majjhima Nikāya* 22](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN22.html), translated
by Ṭhānissaro Bhikkhu. The sections [“The Water-snake Simile”](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN22.html#toc_2)
and [“The Raft Simile”](https://www.dhammatalks.org/suttas/MN/MN22.html#toc_3)
locate the relevant portions of the discourse. The translator's
introduction and notes are commentary accompanying the translated text.

The water-snake passage concerns how a teaching is grasped and understood;
the raft passage follows a traveler who builds and uses a raft to cross, then
considers whether to keep carrying it. Read the complete movement: what the
means makes possible, how it is used, and when carrying it becomes an
unnecessary burden. This gives “letting go” a situation and a purpose.

The raft connects the preceding question of purpose with means: tools,
conceptual models, and the little-fire self-image can contribute to life,
remain revisable, and be released when their work is done. These three discourses
supply the specific sources for this dialogue. Creative cultivation is the
work's contemporary development, while nirvana retains the liberative meaning
stated in the discourses. This reading path leaves room for further inquiry
rather than summarizing Buddhist traditions.

## Nietzsche: three metamorphoses and creation

Nietzsche's [Thus Spoke Zarathustra, Part One, “The Three Metamorphoses”](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/1998/1998-h/1998-h.htm) presents bearing, negation, and new creation through the camel, lion, and child. [What kind of person do we want to cultivate? — Baby Heraclitus](https://boluopai.net/philosophy/baby-heraclitus/en/) already draws on this line: the child concerns beginning and creating anew, not abandoning judgment or prescribing stages everyone must complete.

## Freud: negation and acceptance

Freud's [“Negation” (1925), pp. 1–3](https://lutecium.org/ftp/Freud/pdf/1925_negation.pdf)
distinguishes intellectual recognition and acceptance in an analytic account.
Its interpretive inversions do not license treating ordinary denial as admission.

## Lacan: enunciation, retroaction, and singular creation

Lacan brings three connected questions to this collection: how people acquire a position through language and images, how later speech changes the meaning of earlier expression, and how singular practices participate in a person's mode of existence. The site develops these questions into contemporary accounts of personal modeling, model collaboration, and the sinthome.

Conceptual explanations, examples, formal translations, and original-text references are gathered in [A guide to Lacanian concepts: from original texts to personal practice](lacan-reading-guide.md). Use it to understand the Other, the divided subject, retroaction, the three registers, and the sinthome, then return to the relevant essay. It also distinguishes the original concepts from this site's extensions.

## Fire myths: Prometheus, Kuafu, and living together

Prometheus is a mythic figure; Hesiod and Plato supply different texts through
which this collection encounters him. Hesiod's [*Theogony*, lines 507–616](https://www.theoi.com/Text/HesiodTheogony.html)
gives fire theft and its carrier a narrative form. [*Shan Hai Jing*, Haiwai Beijing](https://zh.wikisource.org/zh-hans/山海經/海外北經)
supplies Kuafu's pursuit. [Stealing fire and cultivating creative capacity](stealing-fire.md) compares pursuing
overwhelming power with bringing usable capacities into a finite life.

Plato's [*Protagoras*, 320c–322d](https://classics.mit.edu/Plato/protagoras.html)
places the myth in Protagoras's account of civic learning: arts and fire are
followed by reverence and justice shared by all. Plato is the dialogue's
author, Protagoras a thinker represented within it, and Prometheus a character
in its myth. The fire essay takes their relation as a comparison between
technical means and purposes of living together. Its AI application is a
contemporary argument, rather than a claim about an ancient prediction.

## Deleuze and Guattari: connections, production, and becoming

The independently authored [Machines and becoming essay](https://boluopai.net/philosophy/deleuze-and-becoming/en/)
reads [*Anti-Oedipus*](https://files.libcom.org/files/Anti-Oedipus.pdf),
chapter 1 and chapter 4's first positive task of schizoanalysis, alongside
[*A Thousand Plateaus*](https://files.libcom.org/files/A%20Thousand%20Plateaus.pdf),
plateaus 4 and 6. Desiring-machines open the question of what connections
produce; assemblages bring bodies and expression into view. The essay
distinguishes a process of becoming from a clinical condition and treats
its songwriting and harness examples as contemporary comparisons.
This provides a neighboring question to the sinthome essay: through what
connections can an established personal practice arrangement produce new capacities?

## Personal practice: Bruner, Foucault, Hobbes, and Mill

Bruner's [*Life as Narrative*](https://ewasteschools.pbworks.com/f/Bruner_J_LifeAsNarrative.pdf)
and Foucault's [*Technologies of the Self*](https://foucault.info/documents/foucault.technologiesOfSelf.en/)
inform [Stealing fire and cultivating creative capacity](stealing-fire.md)'s connection between self-expression,
continuing practice, and change. Narrative can organize later understanding;
writing and relations with others can participate in self-transformation.
The model-supported extension belongs to this collection.

[Personal-first humanism](personal-first-humanism.md) also reads Hobbes's
[*Leviathan*, chapter IV](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Leviathan_%281651%29/Chapter_4)
on names and particular things, and Mill's [*On Liberty*, chapter III](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/34901/34901-h/34901-h.htm)
on individuality and experiments of living. These sources clarify the
questions of description and plural coexistence. The present personal-first
orientation remains a separately argued choice.

## Inquiry and frameworks: Peirce, Dewey, Carnap, and Feng Ye

[Reasoning](reasoning.md) uses Peirce's [1908 manuscript MS 841](https://fromthepage.com/jeffdown1/c-s-peirce-manuscripts/ms-841-1908-a-neglected-argument-text-and-fragments?page=9)
to distinguish explanatory hypotheses, experiential consequences, and assessment.
[Embodied experimental philosophy](embodied-experimental-philosophy.md) reads
Dewey's [*Reconstruction in Philosophy*, chapters IV–V](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/40089/40089-h/40089-h.htm):
experience includes acting and undergoing consequences. Together these routes
give inquiry room to propose, act, return, and revise.

Carnap's [*Empiricism, Semantics, and Ontology*](https://ditext.com/carnap/carnap.html)
distinguishes questions within a framework from its practical adoption.
[Finite inquiry and abstraction](finite-inquiry-and-abstraction.md) compares
this collection's question-relative account with Professor Feng Ye's
[*Studies in No-Self Physicalism*](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-981-19-8143-2),
especially §§1.1, 6.5, and 7.4.2. Their shared methodological questions about
finite understanding and representation do not establish identical accounts
of physical realization. YinYang adopts one physical reality as its common
ground while using question-relative abstraction; particular reductions and
bridge assumptions require their own support. Compare the actual arguments
rather than inferring either agreement or disagreement from their labels.

## Will, irrationality, and self-conflict: Frankfurt, Davidson, Mele, and Holton

These philosophers give conflict more specific objects: desire and endorsement, resolve and later inclination, evidence and motivation, and a person's interpretation of themselves. They are supplementary entrances, not proof that the collection accepts one shared architecture of mind.

| Philosopher and original | Central question |
| --- | --- |
| Harry Frankfurt, [Freedom of the Will and the Concept of a Person (1971)](https://www.jstor.org/stable/2024717) | How does wanting something differ from wanting a particular desire to become one's effective motive? First-order desires and second-order volitions offer one analysis of conflict. |
| Donald Davidson, [Paradoxes of Irrationality (1982)](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511554636.018); [Deception and Division (reprinted in the 2004 collection)](https://doi.org/10.1093/0198237545.003.0013) | How can reasons, causes, and mental partitioning help explain irrationality and self-deception? Partitioning is an explanatory proposal to compare with alternatives. |
| Alfred R. Mele, [Real Self-Deception (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X97000034) | Must self-deception involve intentional self-deception or simultaneous contrary beliefs? Examine motivationally biased evidence processing and the accompanying commentaries and reply. |
| Richard Holton, [Intention and Weakness of Will (1999)](https://web.mit.edu/holton/www/pubs/Weakness.pdf); [What Is the Role of the Self in Self-Deception? (2001)](https://web.mit.edu/holton/www/pubs/s-d.pdf) | Resolve, premature abandonment, reasonable reconsideration after conditions change, and understanding one's own beliefs. Author-manuscript pagination may differ from the journals. |

For conflict in experience, relationships, and therapy, continue to [psychological readings](psychological-readings.md#psychological-conflict-relationships-and-change).

## Lakoff, Johnson, and Gentner: metaphor and relational analogy

George Lakoff and Mark Johnson's [Metaphors We Live By, chapters 1–3](https://press.uchicago.edu/ucp/books/book/chicago/M/bo3637992.html) concerns metaphor in everyday conceptual life. Dedre Gentner's [Structure-Mapping (1983)](https://groups.psych.northwestern.edu/gentner/papers/Gentner83.2b.pdf) makes relational mapping explicit. [Writing a personal philosophy through literary programming](https://boluopai.net/philosophy/literary-programming/en/) and [Metaphor and personal modeling](metaphor-and-self-revision.md) already use these resources. Metaphor gives understanding a form; particular analogical inferences still need checking.

## Mind, knowledge, and explanation

Clark and Chalmers's [*The Extended Mind*](https://www.consc.net/papers/extended.html)
informs [Stealing fire and cultivating creative capacity](stealing-fire.md)'s account of active environmental
supports. [Metaphor and personal modeling](metaphor-and-self-revision.md) connects
that question with Hollan, Hutchins, and Kirsh's distributed-cognition
research: understanding can develop across people, models, records, and tools.

Varela, Evan Thompson, and Eleanor Rosch's [The Embodied Mind (1991)](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262220422/the-embodied-mind/) brings cognitive science, lived experience, and contemplative practice together; [Shared reflection and continued cultivation](participation-and-support.md) already uses it to examine embodied participation. Hollan, Hutchins, and Kirsh's [Distributed Cognition (2000)](https://interactivity.ucsd.edu/articles/dcog.pdf) supplies the original account of cognition across people, artifacts, and time.

Joshua Knobe and Shaun Nichols's [An Experimental Philosophy Manifesto (2008)](https://campuspress.yale.edu/joshuaknobe/files/2016/02/manifesto-1qhrtmk.pdf) introduces an empirical research orientation; the linked author-hosted file is a proof. [Embodied experimental philosophy](embodied-experimental-philosophy.md) distinguishes this tradition from personal philosophical experimentation through making, participation, and reflection.

Gordana Dodig-Crnkovic's [Information, Computation, Cognition (2013)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1311.0413) is another natural-philosophical resource already used in [Stealing fire and cultivating creative capacity](stealing-fire.md): informational structure, computational dynamics, and agency across levels. It neighbors embodied, extended, and distributed cognition without being the same theory or establishing that human experience is a digital algorithm.

For psychological processes, empirical assessment, belief, and interaction, continue to [psychological readings](psychological-readings.md). Their theories and evidence investigate how knowing actually happens; philosophy continues to ask about explanation, grounds for knowledge, and the scope of mind.

Technical sources for the Gödel Machine and Darwin Gödel Machine appear in [engineering readings](engineering-readings.md#experimental-implementation-data-and-model-assessment), alongside implementation and evaluation resources. [Preference harness](preference-harness.md) retains its philosophical comparisons while preserving the assumptions and scope of formal repair, inference, and query transformation.

## Source scope

These links preserve the public intellectual sources and their conceptual
limits. Personal evidence and the history of adopting or revising an
interpretation remain in the situated package's source account. The reading
map does not replace reading the passages that matter to an actual argument.
