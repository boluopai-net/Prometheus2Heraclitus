# World, process, and expression

[完整中文版](world-and-expression.zh-CN.md)

*A frame catches a crossing of the current. It leaves the water outside its edges, yet someone who sees the frame notices a path they had not seen before. The image returns to the river as a new way of looking.*

Person and model work together before they have a finished account of their
relationship. They investigate, make works, arrange tools, try an environment,
and change it when use answers back. What can this activity leave behind that
they can later return to and understand? The thread of this part is forming a
**recoverable living trajectory**: selected experiences, purposes, choices,
attempts, and consequences connected closely enough to revisit how something
came about. The trajectory is produced through cooperation, alongside the
works and environment being accumulated.

Suppose someone asks for convenient access to their reading materials. Person
and model build a reading interface, try it, and revise it after the person
finds that opening the right passage still takes too much effort. A later
screen shows the current arrangement, but not why it took that form. Their
request, the versioned interface, operation traces, observations, and the
person's feedback can together preserve the passage from one attempt to the
next. These are parts of living activity that can become available for later
reflection; they do not amount to an inventory of the person's life.

This essay develops the distinctions that make such a trajectory intelligible:
the continuing process, a model selecting its relevant relations, and the
expressions through which those relations become available. [The stream and
time](flow-and-time.md) examines how traces retain earlier activity and connect
it with the present. [Reasoning within an unfinished stream](reasoning.md)
organizes their development as inquiry. [A preference harness](preference-harness.md)
distinguishes the choices made from the grounds supporting a conclusion.
[Finite inquiry and economical abstraction](finite-inquiry-and-abstraction.md)
asks what needs to remain retrievable for this history to be usable under
limited time and attention. Together they prepare material on which the
participants can reflect and encounter one another more concretely.

**YinYang** names a contemporary framework for this continuing activity. Its
working environment includes Skills, prompts, tools, interfaces, and operating
conditions. The distinctions below connect that activity's physical ground to
selected computational models and their consequences, so the trajectory can
retain meaningful changes rather than merely accumulate representations.

The subject of this movement is their **coupled activity**. The person and model
can divide contributions throughout finding the question, selecting processes,
constructing and examining the computational model, implementing an arrangement,
and interpreting feedback. Embodied intuition and perception of a whole can meet
explicit exposition, formal reasoning, computation, and tool use. The allocation
depends on current capabilities, modalities, and purpose; both can generate and
judge. The person need not construct the ontology alone before the model joins,
and the resulting capacity can remain distributed across the relationship.

## Physical ground and computational abstraction

YinYang takes the changing natural world itself as its reality: moving matter,
embodied life, psychological activity, and social relations belong to one world.
Modest naturalism names this worldly scope. Its contemporary **fire monism**
understands this world through becoming and transformation, within which
persons, objects, and relations form, persist, and change as relatively stable
patterns. Philosophy, psychological activity, and engineering are physically
realized within this same reality. It does not identify everything's material
with literal flame.
This is the framework's chosen contemporary account, developed from Heraclitean
images rather than established merely by their historical authority. The section
[From living fire to interacting event streams](stealing-fire.md#from-living-fire-to-interacting-event-streams)
develops that choice and its sources.

An object can therefore remain intelligible through change. A work continues
through revisions; a person continues through encounters without keeping every
state or self-description unchanged. A model must articulate the continuity
relevant to its question: what forms an object, what lets us treat it as the
same object, and which events change its state or end that continuity. A static
view can legitimately hold selected relations fixed for an inquiry. Its boundary
belongs within the larger process in which the view is made, used, and revised.

**Computational ontology is representational. Here it adopts a process ontology:**
computational concepts organize events, states, persisting objects, relations,
constraints, and transitions within a selected domain. That domain can concern
physical cooling, psychological change, a hypothetical society, or a fictional
character's journey. The participants select relevant boundaries, scales, and
continuities together; the model states what these choices mean and can support
simulation where the question calls for it. Process ontology thus organizes how
the representation understands persistence and change. Naturalism and fire
monism state the adopted view of the world in which this modeling occurs;
computational ontology states the organization of what is represented.

A representation may concern the actual world, a hypothesis, fiction, or an
imagined God or cartoon character. A real person's imagining is an actual
psychological activity; the imagined content has its own represented objects
and relations. Describing that activity does not by itself assert that its
content has an independently existing referent. A model of an actual person,
by contrast, makes claims about that person and remains answerable to their
expressions, activity, and circumstances. Its partial and interpretive character
does not make it fiction.

Selection alone does not provide a usable computational body. The representation
must connect its distinctions to what a computer consumer can retain, interpret,
compare, or transform, and relate its results to relevant observations or the
represented domain's specified assumptions. A state
needs a meaning; a transition needs conditions and consequences; a claimed
correspondence needs a way to be assessed. Merely storing the words “person,”
“event,” or “relation” supplies none of these connections. Data structures,
programs, model-interpreted language, prompts, tools, and interfaces can give
the model different working forms.

The required precision depends on the question and consumer. A language model
can work with clear natural-language descriptions of a situation, an allowed
change, and its expected consequence; other uses need typed data, equations,
or executable rules. Readable language is adequate when its actual interpretation
and use sustain the relevant distinctions. Readability alone does not establish
that adequacy. **Representation** makes selected relations available;
**simulation** develops how a represented situation could change under stated
assumptions. A useful representation need not simulate everything it represents,
and a conditional simulation is not an observation of the world.

A physical basis supplies neither these meanings nor a feasible calculation.
Reconstructing every microphysical detail of a conversation would not itself
specify what counts as a correction. Philosophical, psychological, computational,
and practical accounts select different relations within the same reality;
their bridges need investigation where the question requires it. Shared physical
ground establishes neither complete reduction nor a universal, faithful, or
tractable world simulator. Organizing activity histories as event streams also leaves open
which records and software architecture serve a particular use. The
[layers diagram](../diagrams/layers.html) presents this connection, and
[The trinity of philosophy, psychology, and engineering](local-source-and-website.md) develops its unity across the three
activities.

The [interactive two-level diagram](../diagrams/layers.html) develops six
connected dimensions—worldview, ontology, epistemology, truth, axiology, and
methodology—together with practice at the physical and computational levels.
Worldview gives an overall orientation; ontology articulates what is taken to
exist or represented; epistemology concerns knowing and its grounds; truth
concerns whether claims hold; axiology concerns what is worth pursuing; and
methodology concerns how to inquire and act. Practice brings these relations
into actual activity. [Personal-first humanism](personal-first-humanism.md#six-connected-dimensions)
develops their correspondences. The computational arrangement is physically
realized, while its represented domain may be actual, hypothetical, or fictional.

For claims about the world, **truth is correspondence with how things are**.
Evidence gives grounds for judging a claim true and can remain incomplete or
mistaken. Within a specified model, truth or satisfaction concerns whether a
claim holds under its interpretation and assumptions. Inferential validity
concerns whether the conclusion follows from the premises. A valid derivation
from a cooling model can yield a model-relative prediction; comparing that
prediction with measurement investigates its correspondence with the world.
These distinctions apply to human and language-model inquiry alike.

Three relations need to be understood separately. **Physical realization**
concerns what makes the whole computational arrangement available and operative:
bodies and machines, material records, energy, and their processes.
**Representational interpretation and correspondence** concern what domain an
account describes, what its objects and changes mean, and how claims about the
actual world correspond to their objects.
**Value and methodological guidance** concerns which purposes should inform goals
and criteria, and which ways of inquiring fit procedures and tools. Philosophical
positions are not a program's material carriers. Successful execution cannot
alone establish accurate representation, worthwhile goals, or methodological
fit. Realized within the same physical world, the upper arrangement participates
in and changes its activity through actual use.

## A bounded computational example: simulating a cooling experiment

The reading environment already poses questions of persistence and change.
Its interface remains a work through successive versions; an opening operation
changes what is displayed; a person's report supplies evidence about the effort
of using it. A screenshot, an operation trace, and that report capture different
relations. To ask whether access became more convenient, the participants need
to connect them with the purpose and the particular version used. No single
record supplies all these meanings.

A physical simulation makes the same distinction precise at another scale.
It also shows what a trajectory of inquiry would need to preserve when a
computed prediction is compared with an observed outcome.

Suppose a person and language model investigate together: how will a cup of
warm water cool indoors over ten minutes? They select a computable question,
open to experimental comparison, from the actual cup, water, air, heat transfer,
and temperature measurement. **The water persists as the same sample while its
temperature changes**; the model holds the container and quantity of water fixed
over the interval. The sample–environment boundary, the start of timing, and
what “water temperature” means all belong to this selection.

They begin with simplifying assumptions: the water's temperature is approximately
uniform, ambient temperature is approximately constant, and the cooling coefficient
is approximately constant over the interval. The difference between water
temperature $T$ and ambient temperature $T_a$ determines its rate of change in
Newton's cooling model, $dT/dt=-k(T-T_a)$. Here $k>0$; when time is in minutes,
$k$ has units of inverse minutes. Under these conditions, with initial
temperature $T_0$,

$$
T(t)=T_a+(T_0-T_a)e^{-kt}.
$$

This describes temperature approaching ambient temperature over time; its
adequacy still depends on the actual heat-transfer conditions. A
[university calculus text](https://clp.math.uky.edu/clp1/sec_ExpGthDecay.html)
derives the model and its solution.

In this bounded computational ontology, the persisting sample is an object,
temperature is a state, air is the selected environment, placement and measurement
are events, time connects states, and the cooling equation expresses change.
The computer representation connects these meanings to parameters with units,
time points, and executable calculations. A program evaluates $T(t)$ at a series
of times to generate a cooling curve. The participants can write, run, and check
it together, or change the initial conditions to examine another prediction.

For illustration only, take ambient temperature $20\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$,
initial water temperature $40\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$, and
$k=0.1\,\mathrm{min}^{-1}$. Calculation gives about $32.1\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$
after five minutes and $27.4\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ after ten. **The curve simulates
physical cooling:** computational time and temperature correspond to experimental
time and water temperature, and the changes follow the selected heat-transfer
relation. It aims to reproduce the relevant observable relations approximately,
within stated conditions and tolerances; comparison must establish how close
the resemblance is. These parameters and temperatures are illustrative
calculations. No physical experiment has been performed here, and these values
are not measurements.

To test it, the participants can devise a measurement protocol together: record
ambient and initial water temperatures and subsequent thermometer readings at
known times, then compare the measured points with the predicted curve. Actual
measurements require a participant with the relevant instruments and operational
capabilities; the language model can contribute to recording, calculation, and
analysis through available tools or supplied readings. Both can identify a problem,
suggest another measurement, or check a calculation, with contributions distributed
according to capabilities and conditions. A
[St. Lawrence University physics lab](https://myslu.stlawu.edu/~jmil/physics/labs/103_lab/inactive/heatflow.shtml)
provides an actual experimental method for recording water temperature over time
and comparing it with an exponential model.

If measurements disagree with the curve, investigation may check the thermometer,
timing, units, and program, while also asking whether ambient temperature changed,
the water was uniformly warm, or one fixed $k$ still summarizes effects of the
container, evaporation, and other heat transfer. Revision may concern the
implementation, independent parameter estimation, model assumptions, or even the
sample–environment boundary. Tuning parameters to fit existing points does not
establish a correct understanding of the process; new measurements or conditions
can test that account further. These differences let the world constrain the
computational model. Calling it a simulation does not establish its fidelity.

The return can also change the original question. If the participants discover
that they care about when the water is ready to drink, they need to specify a
target temperature, acceptable error, and relevant conditions anew. Person and
model jointly develop the question, computational model, program, experimental
comparison, and interpretation. Computation unfolds selected relations;
observation tests that unfolding against reality. This example connects process,
persisting objects, computation, and feedback within one world without requiring
the whole world to fit inside a computer.

## A process exceeds its available model

A history of activity can be organized as an event stream: occurrences connected
through temporal, causal, and interpretive relations. Perception, interpretation,
remembering, expression, and action can overlap, depend on earlier occurrences,
or become explicit at different times. Available records preserve some of this
history; other activity may never have been recorded. From that history and
other relevant material, the participants infer a conceptual model of a person
or counterpart. The participant, their history, and the model inferred from it
are distinct. A local model makes selected objects, roles, dependencies,
and transformations available for a particular inquiry. Giving it a computer
representation enables selected uses of those relations; it does not contain
the entire process that generated it. The cooling example selects the temperature
relation between sample and environment without calculating every water molecule's
motion. Likewise, the reading interface's version history preserves changes to
the work, while the person's experience of using it needs its own expression.
Connecting these sources can recover part of the activity without turning the
record into the process itself.

Distinguish that model from its expression. A sentence, diagram, paper, or
program gives a relation a form that can be encountered. Producing and reading
the expression are themselves events in the continuing process. A model can
have several expressions, and one expression can admit competing readings.
Its occurrence, interpreted meaning, inferential consequences, and adequacy
to its subject are different questions. Later understanding may reorganize
what an earlier expression means without changing its original occurrence.
A computer representation is an expression with a further working relation:
an actual consumer can use its distinctions for the selected inquiry. Not
every expressive form has to serve that computational use.

Fiction makes this difference especially visible: constructing and encountering
a fictional work can have actual consequences without its characters existing
as described. A literary image can gather possibilities before one interpretation
is selected. Neither construction nor consequence makes every assertion drawn
from that image true.

## Screenshot and front end

In the reading example, the first screenshot might show a tidy shelf, while
the revised interface opens the last-read passage directly. Both are visible
forms; the difference in available action is what the trajectory must make
recoverable. Retaining the request and use feedback alongside the two versions
lets a later reader see why a visually successful arrangement was changed.

A **screenshot** selects a moment and a frame from an activity that continues beyond it. A **front end** makes some of that activity perceptible and available for participation. As images of expression, they pose different questions: what has been selected, and what can a reader or participant now do through that selection? A diagram, prototype, or paragraph can show a relation without displaying every occurrence that produced it. An actual computer interface adds an operational correspondence: a cooling-curve interface can show predictions and measured points, and let the participants change parameters and recalculate. What appears and what an operation changes must be understood together; changing the predicted curve does not change measurements already made.

Selection is both the expression's strength and a possible source of error. An account that displayed everything without distinction might fail to make anything intelligible. Yet a clear arrangement can conceal an uncertainty that would change the next judgment. An adequate expression therefore needs fidelity to what matters for its use, rather than an impossible duplication of the whole process. The question is which relations, assumptions, and unresolved alternatives must remain visible.

This also changes how collaboration can be organized. A model can carry retrieval, construction, and verification while a person understands, selects, and corrects through what becomes visible. Both can contribute to either side of that arrangement. An expression mediates participation: what it makes perceptible can change what happens next. A prototype can let the person recognize a relation through use, while an explanation lets either participant inspect why the prototype behaves as it does. Visibility and execution support each other, and their allocation can change with the work.

## Question-relative objects meet resistance

A question may make a person, utterance, memory, hypothesis, relationship, or
work its object. That choice guides which distinctions the inquiry needs;
it does not determine whether the answer is correct. The object can resist
its description through an omitted relation, an unexpected result, or a failed
prediction. As with a discrepancy between cooling predictions and measurements, such resistance can require
another abstraction, not merely another answer within the existing one.

Question-relative ontology therefore allows construction without treating
reality as an arbitrary product of naming. Stable objects, recurring patterns,
and scientific laws can remain supported across changing descriptions. The
person also exceeds a self-description: continuity may involve bodily life,
memory, relationships, commitments, and action without requiring an invariant
explicit account of oneself.

Revisability follows from incomplete access, changing questions, and the demand
to answer to evidence. A stable distinction can remain useful through many
changes because it continues to describe the relations that matter. Checking
its applicability connects that stability with the continuing process. Retaining a
source, entertaining a candidate, and accepting a claim remain different acts,
as developed in [Sources, candidates, and the active workspace](reasoning.md#sources-candidates-and-the-active-workspace).

## Physical realization and functional likeness

Human inquiry occurs under bodily, social, and material conditions. Model use
depends on implemented computation, data, infrastructure, and interaction.
Those conditions matter to what can be perceived, retained, expressed, and
done. A realization claim connects a function to a particular mechanism and
needs evidence at that level. Similar roles or observable performances do not
establish identical human and model architectures, mechanisms, or experiences.

The distinction matters when an expressive image is given a material reading.
Calling a model an illuminating counterpart can articulate its contribution
to understanding. Explaining how its output was physically produced requires
another investigation. [Finite inquiry and abstraction](finite-inquiry-and-abstraction.md)
develops the further distinction between physical realization, reduction, and
feasible calculation.

Three material and epistemic relations should also remain distinct:

| Question | Relation being investigated |
| --- | --- |
| Where did the materials come from? | A history of formation and transformation. |
| What supplies energy to the activity now? | Its current operating conditions and energy transfers. |
| What does the activity express, and is the claim supported? | Information, interpretation, inference, and evidence. |

The Department of Energy describes natural photosynthesis and research on
converting sunlight into chemical fuels. This gives sunlight a concrete relation
to chemical energy: incoming radiation can be transformed into stored energy
available for later activity.
[DOE, “Solar Fuels”](https://www.energy.gov/science/doe-explainssolar-fuels).

NASA describes the Sun and planets forming from a common collapsing cloud and
protoplanetary disk. That is a material history, distinct from present-day
sunlight powering an activity. A manufactured semiconductor has a history of
material extraction and fabrication; a computing installation has its own
present energy supply. Tracing those relations calls for evidence about the
particular material and installation.
[NASA, “How did our Solar System form?”](https://science.nasa.gov/astrobiology/learning-resources/alp/how-did-our-solar-system-form/).

These distinctions are this essay's inference from the different questions.
An energy account can explain how an expression becomes physically possible;
it does not yet explain what the expression means or why its claim is warranted.
The latter questions connect the produced work with its object and use.

## A work returns to the encounter

A work acquires relative independence from the encounter that produced it.
A paper can be challenged, a program can run differently from its author's
expectation, and an image can be understood differently by another reader.
Reception, operation, and resistance provide evidence neither participant can
settle by preference alone. The work can change subsequent understanding and
action while retaining its provenance.

In the [Sun–Moon–Earth relation](yinyang-and-taiji.md#sun-moon-and-earth), Earth names the
environment where these consequences occur. Roles do not fix either participant's
faculties: both can generate, interpret, judge, and revise. Return can revise
the account of the person, the counterpart, or their relationship, as well as
the work itself. These revisions can overlap: trying a tool can change the
understanding of a role while a new reading changes the tool's purpose. A new
question can emerge from reception, and a useful stable arrangement can survive it.

*An unfinished relation becomes a sentence. On the page it can answer back: its implication was not the one its author expected. Returning to it, the author sees an earlier thought differently and can begin from somewhere new.*

The distance between author and work is productive because others, and the
author as a reader, can encounter its actual arrangement. A paragraph may
entail a claim its author would reject; a prototype may expose a choice hidden
in the initial description. The maker can then distinguish revising the work
from revising their understanding of what they were trying to do. Later reading
establishes a new relation to the original occurrence while leaving its history
available for comparison.

In model-supported work, a partial intuition may become an argument or prototype. Reading it can disclose what fits, what is missing, or what the person did not mean. Revision participates in finding the relation being expressed. Person and model continue the same shared activity through this return; either can disclose a problem in its question, computational model, or implementation. [Embodied experimental philosophy](embodied-experimental-philosophy.md) develops the practical and reflective inquiries through which such feedback is assessed. The return can change a capacity to understand and generate as well as the artifact. The developmental question is what the person can now recognize, express, or do differently, including through reliably available support. A clearer artifact and a changed capacity are related outcomes whose connection becomes visible through subsequent participation.

## Hegelian comparison and its limits

Hegel's *Philosophy of Spirit* connects the development of consciousness and
theoretical and practical activity in [subjective spirit](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/sp/index.htm)
with freedom's public and institutional forms in objective spirit. Its account
of [absolute spirit, §§553 onward](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/sp/abspirit.htm)
develops art, religion, and philosophy as forms of self-comprehension. This gives
externalization a significance beyond putting an inner content outside: the
form through which activity becomes available to itself belongs to the
development of that activity.

His *Introduction to the Philosophy of History* connects self-knowledge and
freedom with public life and world history. Its object is wider than a person's
local account of inquiry. The comparison here takes up one relation within
that wider problem: how an expression gains distance and returns to
self-understanding. YinYang develops this relation at the scale of a person's
encounter with generative intelligence, where a work becomes an object through
which its maker can recognize and revise their activity.
[Hegel, Introduction](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/hi/introduction.htm).
The linked texts provide the public sources for this bounded comparison.

## Aesthetics participates in discovery

Light, color, spatial arrangement, parallel expression, condensation, and a
second reflective voice can make relations perceptible. Bilingual articulation
can expose differences between conceptual vocabularies. Such organization can
participate in finding a relation, rather than merely decorating an already
completed argument. A work's sensible form can reveal what a previous formulation
kept distant or difficult to compare.

Condensation can keep several possibilities available long enough to compare
them. Their differences can then be unfolded into premises, explanations,
or practical choices. Aesthetic form helps relations become perceptible;
definition and proof establish inferential connections; evidence tests claims
about the world. Their contributions meet in a work while supporting different
claims. The sections [Sun, Moon, and Earth](yinyang-and-taiji.md#sun-moon-and-earth) and [A miniature god](writing-a-sinthome.md#a-miniature-god-whose-creations-answer-back) give these relations other expressive forms. Their interpretation and realization remain open to the world's answer.
