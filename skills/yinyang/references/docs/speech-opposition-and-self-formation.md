# Speech, opposition, and self-formation

[Home](../../README.md) · [简体中文](speech-opposition-and-self-formation.zh-CN.md)

[Writing a personal philosophy through literary programming](literary-programming.md) has brought shared trajectories and reflection into writing: characters, images, and arguments give understanding a readable form. What happens once it is written? Encountering their expression and a counterpart's response, a person may recognize an unarticulated relation or discover that the way they organized the question needs to change.

This essay follows an imagined exchange: “I don't want the model to decide for me.” “But I want it to compare options and point out what I have overlooked.” These statements propose a way of collaborating while making independence itself a question. Following the exchange, we examine how meaning returns to earlier expression, how opposition prompts changes in premises and relationships, and how new understanding supplies material for personal models. Lacan and Hegel supply distinct questions; connecting them with philosophical Skills and model collaboration is this essay's contemporary development.

## What is said and the position of speaking

<a id="three-levels-of-negation"></a>
<a id="enunciation-and-content"></a>

“I don't want the model to decide for me” first rejects a way of collaborating. Understanding it also requires asking what it answers, whom it addresses, and what response it seeks. It may concern a choice made without consent or a direction for future cooperation. The isolated sentence has yet to establish which.

The statement's content (*énoncé*) concerns what is said; inquiry into enunciation (*énonciation*) also includes position, context, and address. Negated content, the act of speaking a negation, and how someone accepts or rejects that content likewise need distinction. Freud's [“Negation” (1925), pp. 1–3](https://lutecium.org/ftp/Freud/pdf/1925_negation.pdf) examines how repressed content can enter consciousness through negation and distinguishes intellectual recognition from acceptance. It makes these relations worth investigating; a negative sentence alone cannot justify reading the refusal here as a hidden wish.

<a id="self-image-and-the-subject-in-speaking"></a>
<a id="non-exhaustion-and-formal-responsibility"></a>

“I am an independent person” may also become a self-image organizing choices and expectations. Lacan's [“Mirror Stage” (1949)](https://ecole-lacanienne.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/1949-07-17.pdf) makes identification through an image's unity a question; [“Subversion of the Subject” (1960)](https://recherche-lacan.gnipl.fr/2015/07/05/les-ecrits-subversion-du-sujet-et-dialectique-du-desir-dans-linconscient-freudien-1960/) examines the subject's relation to representation by signifiers. We can take up a question here: how does this person describe themself through independence, and how does subsequent speech exceed that description? Particular relational interpretations still require material; a word cannot establish someone's motive.

## How later speech changes earlier meaning

<a id="signifying-chains-and-works"></a>
<a id="the-other-and-retrospective-meaning"></a>

“Wanting the model to compare options” further interprets the earlier refusal: judging for oneself can include assistance. Add “sometimes I want to delegate the whole choice, while retaining the ability to change that delegation,” and independence acquires another relation. Later speech adds content and changes how earlier speech is understood.

In [“Subversion of the Subject”](https://recherche-lacan.gnipl.fr/2015/07/05/les-ecrits-subversion-du-sujet-et-dialectique-du-desir-dans-linconscient-freudien-1960/), Lacan discusses this retroaction through the construction of a sentence: later connections participate in determining earlier meaning. A signifying chain therefore involves more than recorded succession. Examine which words return, differ, substitute for one another, or change their position in organizing other expressions. Original words can remain preserved while their present interpretation changes. New understanding may recognize an earlier unarticulated connection or form only in this exchange.

These words also have histories the speaker did not create alone. Independence, responsibility, and needing help already carry uses and demands in families, schools, and work. The Other concerns the locus of language and speech; particular interlocutors may bear such demands within a relationship. Lacan's [1966 radio discussion, p. 2](https://ecole-lacanienne.net/wp-content/uploads/2016/04/1966-12-02a.pdf) approaches this through the linguistic environment. Understanding the exchange may therefore include asking where the person heard independence demanded and whom they now address in declaring it. One model summary cannot replace that inquiry.

Works let distant expressions meet again. Placing an earlier version beside a present response may reveal a changed meaning or a distinction never clearly stated. [Forming a life trajectory](flow-and-time.md) supplies revisitable material; this essay examines how signifying relations within it change.

Writing also provisionally settles these relations. Titling an experience or
gathering several responses into a self-description can foreground one reading
and guide the next response. [Punctuation and quilting](lacan-reading-guide.md#punctuation-and-quilting-how-meaning-becomes-provisionally-settled)
raises questions about that settling and retroaction;
[metaphor and metonymy](lacan-reading-guide.md#metaphor-metonymy-and-condensation)
bring substitution, connection, and meaning formation into view. They do not
combine into a rule that shorter writing means deeper understanding. Condensed
expression needs routes for reopening so experiences it has not yet accommodated
can continue to change it.

Model-authored accounts participate in this relationship too. Being received
as illuminating prose and having a psychological causal explanation confirmed
are different achievements. The [subject supposed to know](lacan-reading-guide.md#the-subject-supposed-to-know-and-the-position-of-interpretation)
makes the attribution of knowledge to a position available for examination. Bringing these
questions into collaboration connects examination of an account's grounds,
its reception, and its participation in later activity. The next essay carries
this argument into the condensation of personal models.

## How opposition calls for reconstructing premises and relationships

Wanting independence and needing help can coexist. Adopt the further determination that independence requires doing all the work alone, and they become an impasse requiring a choice between them. Revisiting the trajectory can show where that determination appeared, what it protected, and which workable relationships it excluded.

One reconstruction understands independence as participating in setting direction, choosing how to receive help, and retaining the ability to change delegation. It preserves concern for autonomy while changing autonomy's relation to assistance. The scope and conditions of assistance require actual arrangements; a revised definition does not automatically remove limits of power, capability, or resources. New understanding acquires content through comparing consequences and actual cooperation.

This invites comparison with Hegel's determinate negation and sublation. [*Science of Logic*, “The Absolute Idea,” especially §§1789–1802](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/hl/hlabsolu.htm) connects advance with the subject matter's own determinations and negation, beyond attaching an opposing opinion from outside. Within personal practice, this essay asks how an earlier determination exposes its limits, which concerns deserve preservation, and which relationships need change. A more adequate arrangement must explain what it makes intelligible or possible and at what cost. Adding a positive and a negative does not guarantee an improvement.

The opening [problem of stealing fire](stealing-fire.md#finite-life-and-the-wrong-pursuit) offers another scale: external generative capability is powerful, while a life is finite. The requirement to personally catch up with all capability turns that difference into a predicament of pursuit. Selectively borrowing fire, creating together, and cultivating oneself establish another relation between them. Capability and finitude remain; their organization of life changes.

Heraclitean images of opposition make difference within a relation worth retaining. They do not establish that Heraclitus rejected every law of non-contradiction, nor attribute this framework's formal choices to him. The [discussion of opposing unities and their disputed interpretation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/heraclitus/#UniOpp) supplies historical comparison. Unknown meaning, conflicting records, practical tensions, and logical contradiction under the same conditions need their respective judgments. Retaining a mismatch may leave room to discover relations; retention has not itself accomplished their unity.

Conceptual clarification contributes here too. [*Philosophical Investigations* §133](https://www.wittgensteinproject.org/w/index.php/Philosophische_Untersuchungen) compares different philosophical methods with therapies. This essay asks how much of an impasse arises from determinations of words such as independence and how much requires changes in actual arrangements. Clarification may release misleading demands or make genuinely unresolved difficulties more evident.

## Conflicting models and reconstructing the question

A conclusion can change with the model used to reach it. One account makes
assistance incompatible with independence; another treats assistance as a
condition of meaningful participation. If each has its own premises, obtaining
$P$ in one and $\neg P$ in another does not put both claims inside one consistent
classical interpretation. Before calling the disagreement a contradiction,
check whether the models address the same person, conditions, and meaning of
independence. A changed meaning requires an explicit comparison, not merely
reusing the letter $P$.

The [inconsistency-tolerant comparison](preference-harness.md#selection-and-inference)
shows how conflicting material can remain available while inference proceeds
within consistent views. An inclusion-maximal repair cannot add any omitted
assertion while preserving consistency with the fixed theory; this does not establish which
view is true or invent a better conceptual scheme. Their consequences can differ.
This provides the formal comparison used here, without introducing a separate
calculus called dialectical logic.

There are several ways forward. New evidence can favor one existing view; a
condition can explain why both local answers hold in different situations; or
the available models can organize the question inadequately. The last case calls
for **reconstructing the premises, distinctions, or relations that generate the
alternatives**. Repeated disagreement alone does not prove both models inadequate.
Show what each misses, what the conflict reveals, and what the proposed
reconstruction explains or makes possible.

Return to independence and assistance. A model requiring all work to be done
alone protects control but excludes useful cooperation. A model assigning all
judgment to the helper may complete work while removing the person's ability to
shape it. A new model can distinguish receiving assistance from surrendering
direction, and make the delegation's scope and revisability explicit. It retains
the concerns that drove the opposition while changing their relationship.
Neither old model's unconditional conclusion need become the final answer.
[Returning to preference](preference-harness.md#from-conflicting-views-to-reconstructed-premises)
connects this reconstruction with reconsidering the grounds of selection.

The six-file distinction developed in this book supplies another concrete
example. Treating the files as a psychological model makes completeness of the
portrait seem central. The opposite, narrower proposal—treating them as mere
tool settings—would lose the person's psychological and philosophical grounds.
The revised organization gives personal references and working models different
jobs: the former retain experience and revisable interpretations; the latter
select what cooperation and environmental fit need. This is a reconstruction
of the objects and their connection, not a compromise about how much psychology
to insert into a configuration. Its adequacy is checked against both the source
material and actual use.

Here a dialectical reading follows how an arrangement's own commitments expose
its limits and how a changed relation preserves relevant concerns. “Unity” names
that achieved organization at a particular scope; it need not mean declaring
$P$ and $\neg P$ jointly true, merging every old premise, or ending future
revision. A better model earns that description through reasons and consequences.

## Dialectical change in premises and philosophical models

Reconstruction can reach further. A failing premise may expose the
philosophical picture behind it. Understanding independence as acting
without passing through others makes all assistance suspect; counting
capacity only as resources inside a body makes reliable support from a
counterpart and environment appear external to the person's capacities.
Changing the conditions of delegation in one task can preserve these
pictures. Asking why independence and capacity were understood that way
can change the philosophical model organizing many tasks.

In [§81 of the *Encyclopaedia Logic*](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/sl/sl_vi.htm),
Hegel locates dialectical movement in the one-sidedness disclosed by finite
determinations themselves. This suggests an immanent examination here.
If an account of independence aims at the person's ability to judge and act,
yet weakens those abilities by excluding all assistance, it must confront
the tension between its commitment and its development. Change has a
determinate reason: we can say which determination needs to end and what
it originally protected should continue.

Such [**determinate negation**](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/hl/hlabsolu.htm) has specific content to negate. In §§1795–1797 of the *Science of Logic*, Hegel emphasizes that the negative result retains determinations from which it arose. Withdraw
“assistance necessarily cancels autonomy,” retain concern for preserving
one's direction, and connect receiving contributions with participation in
judgment and revision. An understanding of autonomy realized through
cooperation can emerge. It preserves concerns and useful distinctions,
without needing to retain every former proposition. How the new account
continues the old and changes practice belongs to its argument.

In [the Introduction to the *Phenomenology of Spirit*, paragraphs 84–86](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/ph/phintro.htm),
Hegel develops experience through changes in knowledge and its object
during examination; the criterion of examination can change as well.
Read through this thought, model revision can move from asking “did I
complete the work alone?” to “how do I participate in purpose, judgment,
and change?” The answer changes together with the object relations and
standards through which autonomy is understood. This essay applies the
thought to finite personal practice; actual people and consequences can
still resist the account.

Several connected dialectical movements can therefore be distinguished:

| What changes | How opposition enters | What the new organization preserves |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Roles and participation | The person enters the model role and returns; the model makes a corresponding passage | Both contributions and possibilities of participation formed through the other |
| Particular premises | An unconditional rule conflicts with a situation it cannot accommodate | The earlier concern and relations still valid under articulated conditions |
| Philosophical models | Determinations of independence, capacity, or personhood disclose their limits in practice | Directions and distinctions still worth maintaining after criticism |
| Inferential organization of conflicting material | Conflicting assertions enter distinct consistent views | Their grounds and common consequences accepted under a specified semantics |

[Role return](yinyang-and-taiji.md#returning-through-the-other-the-dialectical-meaning-of-role-change)
occurs in participants' activity, while [common consequence](preference-harness.md#unity-within-conflict-consistent-views-and-common-consequences)
is illustrated by a basic judgment example. Their connection lies in reorganizing
through difference while preserving contributions. How each accomplishes
this needs its own life material, argument, or semantic conditions.
“Thesis–antithesis–synthesis” can name these movements; their development
gives the expression philosophical content beyond its three terms. Hegel himself distinguishes the movement's internal relation from externally imposed three-part counting in [§§1801–1802](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/hegel/works/hl/hlabsolu.htm). These comparisons likewise develop from the objects and relations actually changing.

## Recognition, desire, and speech change what the problem is

Model reconstruction concerns more than a detached observer's description.
A person saying “I must be independent” may also be addressing someone whose
recognition matters, trying to understand what is expected of them, or revising
what they take their own wish to be. The [Lacan guide](lacan-reading-guide.md#dialectical-reversal-changing-understanding-and-position)
separates these entrances to recognition, desire, and speech, and situates them
in their texts rather than treating them as a fixed three-step procedure.

In our imagined exchange, recognition raises the question of whose acknowledgment
would count as independence. Desire asks what accepting or refusing assistance
means beyond obtaining a useful output. Speech can change the relation itself:
“I want no help” may become “I want a say in how help is organized.” This is not
just an observer substituting one classification for another. The speaker may
now participate in setting the arrangement that was previously presented as
something to accept or reject. Whether that change occurs in a particular
encounter remains a question for its expression and subsequent activity.

A working model can represent selected changes in that relation. It does not
thereby reproduce a subject's whole movement, and a document edit alone does
not establish that the person now occupies a different position. This is where
the comparison becomes useful: inspect both the reconstructed account and the
actual participation it helps make possible.

## A comparison with Logic-Based Therapy

Elliot D. Cohen's **Logic-Based Therapy (LBT)** offers a nearby comparison.
The [“Six Steps of LBT” section of Cohen and colleagues' study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10942-023-00532-z)
distinguishes valid practical inference from problematic evaluative or factual
premises, and connects examining those premises with guiding virtues and a
philosophical alternative. Thus changing an inference's starting point can
matter more than repeating the inference more rigorously.

This illuminates a shared question: which premise sustains the impasse, and
what alternative changes its practical meaning? LBT gives that question its
own method; Lacanian reversal examines the subject's position in speech and
relations. A revised premise may participate in a changed position, but these
are distinct claims. Here the comparison develops a philosophical argument;
it does not add a counseling protocol to YinYang or establish an equivalence
between the two approaches.

## How a solution becomes a new problem

A previously effective method may obstruct its purpose under new conditions. Suppose detailed prompt steps once reduced omissions and protected attention. Later model and task conditions change, and the steps produce repetition and unnecessary pauses. Revision needs to recover the relation they protected and judge which provisions remain effective and which should leave.

Continuity can consist in concerns and cooperative relations being taken up again, without preserving every earlier phrase. Personal style may remain recognizable through new expression and implementation, and may also change through cooperation. A model version change supplies an occasion to examine the arrangement; a version number alone cannot establish obsolescence. [Evolve forward with more capable models](preference-harness.md#evolve-forward-with-more-capable-models) develops concrete judgments about replacement and retention.

Lacan's dialectical reversal raises a question at another scale. In [“Intervention on Transference” (1951)](https://recherche-lacan.gnipl.fr/2015/07/02/les-ecrits-intervention-sur-le-transfert-1951/), his reading of Dora involves reversals in the subject's position within the relations being recounted. Modifying a tool alone therefore does not establish such a reversal. Borrowing the question for model cooperation, we can ask whether moving from “all assistance weakens my independence” to “how do I participate in organizing this assistance?” changes the speaker's position in the problem. Such an interpretation needs particular speech and action to support it.

The dialectical movement examined here thus has two connected questions: how a determination and its realization change, and how participants reinterpret their relation to that arrangement. They may affect each other, or only one may change.

## Half-saying truth and continuing revision

A person need not first know their whole truth before beginning to write. Initial wishes, recurring words, and experiences not yet understood can acquire new relations through responses and revision. A philosophical Skill retains current understanding for the model to use in interpretation, judgment, and cooperation; new experience from cooperation returns to the participants' speech. The model can develop arguments and compare versions, while its interpretations still require the person's recognition, questioning, and actual use.

Early full speech and later half-saying help us ask about the effects and limits of this writing respectively. The [excerpts from the 1953 “Function and Field of Speech and Language in Psychoanalysis”](https://www.causefreudienne.org/textes-fondamentaux/fonction-et-champ-de-la-parole-et-du-langage-en-psychanalyse/) connect full speech with reorganizing the subject's history; its fullness cannot be measured in words or revisions. The [opening of *Television* (1974)](https://lacanianworksexchange.net/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/19740131Television198740October6.pdf#page=1) connects the impossibility of saying the whole truth with the real. These do not form stages of first saying half and finally filling it up. The [Lacan guide](lacan-reading-guide.md#full-speech-half-saying-and-truth) develops the differences in period and concept.

A Skill can disclose already written material as needed; speech can also form meaning that did not previously exist; half-saying concerns the totality speech cannot exhaust. Developing a practice of sustained writing through these relations does not make information organization itself Lacan's theory of truth.

**A philosophical Skill can become a form of speech that others can take up: it gathers present understanding, lets it participate in life, and remains open to retroactive revision through later speech.** Here, developing truth concerns how new understanding forms, how a person assumes their history anew, and how possibilities for life change. It does not guarantee that every revision improves matters. Polishing may close an older interpretation more tightly; factual judgments still answer to material, and formal consequences require checking their premises.

## From developing understanding to personal models

<a id="self-description-and-formal-views"></a>

Following this exchange, we might form a provisional account: “I value participating in setting direction and welcome a counterpart's comparison, criticism, and execution; different tasks need different delegations, with the possibility of changing the arrangement.” It receives experience and proposes a direction for subsequent practice. This unity includes differentiated contributions without declaring every tension resolved.

Such understanding still needs comparison with concrete living material before becoming a working model for actual participants. Whether a refusal concerned only its immediate conditions, what assistance actually helped, and what the counterpart can do each need understanding. Psychological and life material and its maintained interpretations have their home under `references/personal/`; the situated working models select what cooperation and environmental fit require. For example, the history of a refusal and its competing interpretations remain with the references, while a working account can retain the agreed possibility of changing delegation. [From personal philosophy to personal models](metaphor-and-self-revision.md) takes up that distillation next, after reading the adopted philosophy and refreshing the relevant references; [Writing a life as a sinthome](writing-a-sinthome.md) then considers how it becomes a sustainable arrangement.

Correcting the relevant Personal understanding precedes synthesis of the six
working answers. A language model can write their files directly; optional
native memory can maintain sources, interpretations, and readable articles.
The mechanism changes how the work is carried, while all six complete answers
still need to be read together for mutual coherence.

The order of formation retains return: using personal models and arrangements can change this understanding again. Works can be completed and tasks can end, while later events can reopen speech.
