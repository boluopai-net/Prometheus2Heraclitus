# Trinity: Philosophy, Psychology, and Engineering in Shared Practice

[Home](../../README.md) · [简体中文](local-source-and-website.zh-CN.md)

The preceding part moved from shared trajectories and reflection on cooperation to personal philosophy, distilled person, counterpart, and relationship models, and a usable sinthome realized in an environment. The question now turns to how that arrangement continues: how local use, maintained source, and readable works stay connected, and how new experience changes them. The contributions of philosophy, psychology, and engineering that formed it also revise one another through continuing practice.

How does someone understand the life they want to live, come to know their own experience, and turn that understanding into usable arrangements? These three questions call for contributions from philosophy, psychology, and engineering. They meet within the same activity: a tool may run yet fail to suit the person; a detailed personal understanding may leave their circumstances unchanged; a claim about living may reveal problems when put to use.

This essay uses “Trinity” to explain the relation among these contributions. **At the general level, they are all activities within the same physical reality; at the individual level, they jointly participate in a particular person's life and practice.** Their shared physical ground places them in one world; their influence on one another in actual work explains why they need to be discussed together.

## The general relation and its individual instance

The naturalism adopted here holds that philosophical thinking, psychological activity, and engineering practice all occur in the physical world. Thinking about what is worth pursuing, experiencing pleasure or difficulty, and modifying a program all involve actual bodies, environments, and activities. Engineering does not finally add physical realization to the other two; all three already occur in reality.

Occurring in one world does not make the three kinds of explanation interchangeable. Describing the physical conditions of a thought has yet to establish whether its reasons are sufficient; knowing that someone likes a response has yet to establish whether it serves their purpose; an executable program is not necessarily worth executing. The questions philosophy, psychology, and engineering each raise still need the corresponding arguments, material, and checks.

| Level | What “one” refers to | What “three” refers to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General relation | The physical reality in which all three activities occur | Philosophical thinking and argument, psychological activity and its study, and engineering that constructs and changes actual conditions. |
| Individual instance | This person and their connected life and practice within the same reality | Their philosophical understanding; their experience, emotions, preferences, and relational activity; and models, tools, and environments organized for actual use. |

The individual instance establishes no further whole outside the physical world. It simply focuses attention on this person: what they have experienced, what they care about, whom they are working with, which tools they use, and how these activities affect their life. Other people and language models can participate too. “Personal” identifies whose life the arrangement serves; it does not require that person to supply every capability and carry out every operation alone.

## How three contributions meet in one activity

Imagine someone repeatedly changing their mind while shopping. They want the model to help compare products, but find that more information makes choosing harder. Adding tools or data alone may not resolve the problem.

The philosophical question is: what is this choice for? Reaching a good-enough decision quickly and enjoying the discovery of different possibilities are both defensible purposes, but they call for different arrangements. “Greater efficiency” cannot by itself choose the person's purpose for them.

Psychological understanding examines how the person actually participates. Are they missing crucial evidence? Afraid to give up other options? Or does exploration itself bring pleasure? These interpretations affect the help needed, but repeated choosing does not establish any one of them. Existing expressions, concrete circumstances, and the person's reactions to different responses can help both participants compare these interpretations.

Engineering turns the adopted understanding into actual conditions. If scattered evidence is the difficulty, the arrangement can organize a few candidates, clarify their differences, and retain the comparison. If exploration belongs to the purpose, it can offer more directions while making earlier options easy to revisit. Retrieval, records, and interface design are thus informed by the other two contributions.

Results in use may overturn these judgments. A concise comparison making choice easier may support the earlier interpretation about information burden; it may also leave the person feeling restricted too soon. The latter result calls for reconsidering the personal understanding, the tools, or even the purpose that “this choice should be settled quickly.” **The three work together through these concrete changes: purpose informs the arrangement, an understanding of the person informs the arrangement, and its consequences change both.**

This does not prescribe philosophy first, psychology second, and engineering last. Inquiry can begin with a wish, a difficulty, an already usable tool, or an unexpected consequence of using it. The three contributions repeatedly intertwine as they address the same question.

## Process ontology and computational expression

This site's process ontology concerns becoming and change: how people, works, and relationships form, persist, and change. Its subject is the world, rather than a technique for organizing files. A person's choices today connect to yesterday's, but this continuity does not require every judgment to stay the same.

A computational ontology expresses objects, events, states, and relations selected from reality for a particular question. In the earlier example, it might represent a choice, the options examined, the point at which the person changed their mind, and the new information available then. These objects are selected so a computer can retain, compare, or process relations relevant to the question; they do not convert the person's whole experience into data.

The connection concerns how the represented events are understood. Treating a change of mind simply as two conflicting properties may overlook that they occurred at different times and answered different information. Understanding it as a process requires explaining what happened before and after, and what changed. Such distinctions can enter natural-language records, structured data, or programs.

Person and language model jointly raise the question, select relations to express, and check whether the expression is useful. Process ontology does not automatically supply a correct personal model, and computational representation does not automatically explain the person in the records. Their concrete connection is established through selected objects, interpretation, and use. [World, process, and expression](world-and-expression.md#physical-ground-and-computational-abstraction) develops this relation and uses a cooling experiment to distinguish representation, simulation, and observation.

## Revising personal understanding and counterpart arrangements through use

An already formed personal model remains answerable to new experiences, works, choices, and interactions. The earlier philosophy expresses an understanding of the world, value, and living; actual use can disclose its conditions of applicability, contradictions, or aspects yet to be articulated. Maintenance compares earlier understanding with new material, asking whether the change concerns the person, counterpart, environment, or their interpretation.

This understanding can have different depths. A preference model begins by describing the options, responses, and working styles the person likes; a psychological model further proposes explanations of how experience, emotion, desire, and relationships affect their participation. The former may already support an arrangement; the latter may help when recurring difficulties or changes need explanation. The current question determines which is needed.

The needed counterpart must be understood alongside the person. What does the person want the model to investigate, judge, and carry out? What capabilities does the available model actually have, and which tools can it use? Understanding the person and the counterpart jointly determines the collaboration. Wanting open exploration, for example, does not mean the model can only agree: it can still identify insufficient evidence, an incoherent explanation, or an unworkable means.

Prompts, Skills, and harnesses bring these understandings into actual work. Prompts express relevant purposes, context, and contributions; Skills organize reusable methods and resources; a harness arranges models, tools, records, and execution conditions together. The existence of files is not the final result. What matters is whether the model can use them to complete the needed work and whether the person can understand, use, and change the arrangement.

## Supplying needed contributions through use

<a id="philosophy-psychology-and-engineering-develop-the-missing-connection"></a>

An arrangement usable for earlier tasks may lack a contribution when it enters new work. New experience can call for developing a relationship left implicit in the philosophy; a new counterpart can need a more explicit personal account; a new tool can change a previously workable division of contributions. Maintenance begins with the actual difference and supplies the understanding or capability needed; existing parts that still fit can continue working.

If the aim is a model environment that actually works, basic engineering capability is necessary, but person and model can supply it together. Retrieving material, organizing an account, implementing tools, and checking effects need not all become prerequisites that the user must personally learn and complete. Supply whichever contribution is missing in relation to the current purpose, rather than arranging the three fields into a curriculum everyone must finish.

For example, an existing model records a preference for receiving the conclusion first, but continued use reveals that this order closes possibilities too early when exploring unfamiliar questions. The participants can narrow the preference's scope, change the relevant responses, and examine the new arrangement's effects. A local revision can retain existing philosophy and tools; deeper theory and more detailed personal models should answer questions that actually arise.

## The local environment, shared source, and website

<a id="three-places-for-the-work-to-continue"></a>

Philosophy, psychology, and engineering are three contributions; the local environment, shared source, and website are places where this work acquires different uses. In actual use, all three contributions run through these places: a website can discuss engineering, a program can realize a philosophical choice, and a local environment contains both personal understanding and actual tools. Recognizing this interweaving still allows us to select their salient roles as images that illuminate one another.

The local environment brings arrangements into daily activity. Whether records can be retrieved, relevant accounts used by the model, and tools made to perform an operation determines whether an understanding actually changes the collaboration. Personal material retains concrete experiences, purposes, and situations of use here.

Shared source makes the reusable parts available for others to obtain, inspect, and modify. This site currently uses GitHub to retain this material. Others can compare versions, object, adopt a method, or make a different arrangement under their own conditions. What is reusable is the selected thought, method, and realization, rather than the original author's complete private portrait.

The website organizes this content for reading and discussion. It can bring arguments, diagrams, and examples together, helping readers understand why an arrangement was designed as it was. On rereading, the author may also discover a previously unnoticed gap. Through this understanding and exchange, the website participates in practice: readers' questions can change an essay's explanation or prompt the author to reconsider the actual arrangement.

The same work can thus have different expressions: an essay develops reasons, a working model condenses the personal understanding currently relevant, and instructions and tools support action. They need not say the same things, but they need to concern actually connected objects. If an essay advocates letting the model undertake investigation while the actual instructions make it wait for the user to supply every detail, the resulting conflict affects use.

## How experience changes understanding and arrangements

<a id="keeping-the-loop-current"></a>

Once a view is used in practice, it encounters unanticipated situations. A model may misunderstand personal preferences, a program may fail to provide the support envisaged, or the person may discover through use that they want something else. What needs revision may be the tool, the personal model, or the original philosophical judgment.

Public exposition and private understanding can therefore revise each other. A relation recognized while writing can help reinterpret personal experience; concrete experience may also reveal that a public claim is too strong. Private material retains the grounds within this life, while a public essay can develop the general question worth discussing. The person still chooses what to disclose.

The “loop” here means letting practical consequences return to earlier judgments: what understanding was adopted, what was done on that basis, what happened, and what changed as a result. It guarantees no steady improvement. Its point is to give unsuitable understandings and arrangements a chance to be discovered and revised. [Embodied experimental philosophy](embodied-experimental-philosophy.md) further considers how actual action and reflection can investigate these changes.

## Trinity and sinthome

The preceding sections explain through actual work how philosophy, psychology, and engineering enter into one another. Returning to this person's life, several sets of images can gather these differences and connections into one picture. The unity remains this person and their connected life-practice within the same physical reality; each row below expresses the three contributions from a different side.

| Philosophy | Psychology | Engineering |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Worldview | Epistemology | Methodology / realization |
| Website | Local environment | GitHub |
| Sun | Moon | Earth |
| Real | Imaginary | Symbolic |
| Father | Son | Holy Spirit |

The first row brings together an understanding of the world and life, the process of knowing oneself and others through experience, and methods that give understanding practical consequences. The second connects this movement to its current forms: the website develops thought for reading and discussion, the local environment brings it into contact with personal experience, and GitHub gives its realization a maintainable form that others can continue. Thought enters life through expression and realization; experience and the consequences of use return to thought. Movement between the columns matters more than fixing each object in one column.

[Sun, Moon, and Earth](yinyang-and-taiji.md#sun-moon-and-earth) give this movement a celestial image: philosophy is like the Sun, proposing directions for understanding the world and life; psychology is like the Moon, giving those directions a personal shape through reception, response, and shaping; engineering is like Earth, making relations into usable conditions that also encounter resistance. This row selects aspects of three activities. In the encounter between person and model, Sun and Moon can also express participants' differentiated contributions; that does not confine either participant to one domain.

Borrowing the three registers from Lacan's [“The Symbolic, the Imaginary, and the Real”](https://www.freud-lacan.com/documents-ged/le-symbolique-limaginaire-et-le-reel/), philosophy turns toward a real not exhausted by images and symbols, psychology gives self and others an image, and engineering writes relations into language, rules, and programs. Father, Son, and Holy Spirit offer another image of three differentiated yet connected aspects. **These correspondences are selected analogies in this essay, not identities between the terms in each column.** Philosophy also needs epistemology and method, psychology has its own understanding of the world, and engineering can change how we know. The analogies bring a relation into view rather than separating these activities.

The Borromean knot further gathers these differences into a mutually sustaining whole. Here the three rings represent personal philosophy, psychology, and engineering. [Forming a sinthome](writing-a-sinthome.md) names the concrete arrangement they jointly shape a “sinthome,” marked by a fourth ring. It includes understanding of the person, model counterpart, and their relationship, together with expressions, tools, and ways of using them that make collaboration possible. Formed through all three contributions, it returns through use to organize them; the connections between rings express formation, sustaining, and feedback. This borrows the image of linkage without yet providing a topological model of this personal practice.

The two essays' objects are connected in this way. The general relation acquires concrete content in this person's life; the concrete arrangement can in turn change their understanding, experience, and working conditions. Its personal character comes from the life it serves, the material on which it draws, and actual participation, beyond merely placing someone's name in a file.

Philosophy, psychology, and engineering have their own questions and can also change one another's work. Bringing them together connects an understanding of living with actual life: a claim can inform action, an experience can change a judgment, and a realization can make something previously unavailable possible.

This connection makes the formed arrangement maintainable and gives revision a concrete object. [Embodied experimental philosophy](embodied-experimental-philosophy.md) next asks which changes in continuing use support modifying a realization, which call for reconsidering a personal model or philosophy, and which can show development in the understanding and creation sought at the opening.
