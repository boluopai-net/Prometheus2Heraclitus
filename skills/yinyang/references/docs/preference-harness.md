# How preference participates in choice and reasoning

[Home](../../README.md) · [Core views](core-views.md#yy-10) · [简体中文](preference-harness.zh-CN.md)

A person encounters more accounts, impressions, and possibilities than they can settle at once. Some conflict; others remain uncertain. To continue, they judge which grounds to work with and which possibilities to develop. Preference participates in that judgment before it necessarily becomes an explicit account of what they prefer.

YinYang understands this preference through the person's activity with models and an environment. Models develop possibilities and carry choices into works; software, tools, and arrangements give those choices a continuing form. Their history can make a selection pattern visible even when the person has never described it. Once visible, that pattern can itself become something to question and change.

## Selection and inference

Description-logic inconsistency-tolerant semantics offers a precise comparison for a basic judgment. A knowledge base separates general axioms $T$ from assertions $A$. If the assertions conflict under those axioms, a repair retains an inclusion-maximal subset consistent with $T$. Priorities among conflicting assertions can affect which repairs are considered. A separate answer rule determines what follows from those selected grounds. A repair is an assertion set; it is not one interpretation or possible world.

For a small example, suppose $P$ and $N$ are disjoint, each implies $Q$, and the sources contain both $P(a)$ and $N(a)$. The repairs keep one or the other. Each entails $Q(a)$, so that conclusion survives both grounds. A preference selecting only the first also makes $P(a)$ available as a conclusion from the selected grounds. That choice does not by itself establish which description fits the actual object. The distinction follows the repair and query definitions in [Bienvenu and Bourgaux, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2003.05746v5#S3).

This comparison concerns the structure of judgment: what is provisionally accepted as a basis, and what that basis warrants. It does not describe how a person calculates. In ordinary inquiry, judging source reliability, choosing what matters for a task, and preferring an outcome can all affect selection, but they supply different reasons. Wanting an outcome does not make evidence for it stronger. When evidence cannot settle everything, one can work from a qualified selection, retain several alternatives, or suspend a particular conclusion while continuing elsewhere.

Here a **working view** means the relevant grounds and relations currently used for a question. It can be expressed in ordinary language. Calling it a view does not make it a formal repair; that comparison applies only when assertions, axioms, consistency, and selection have actually been specified.

## Preference extended through the environment

A personal environment can participate in preference rather than merely transmit a preference already complete inside the person. Someone values sustained reading, asks a model to reorganize a library, adopts direct links, and keeps revising the interface through use. The resulting ability to select and continue reading is realized through the person, model, documents, and software together. The arrangement helps determine what is noticed, chosen, and made possible next.

In this sense, model-assisted activity can be **extended human preference in operation**. A person need not know every implementation detail for a fitting arrangement to carry their direction. The object of understanding is this situated way of selecting, including its tools and conditions; stripping the environment away would remove part of what is being understood.

An enacted arrangement, its interpretation, and the person's present endorsement remain distinguishable within that same relation. A default may have become a useful habit, or may now be exposed as an unwanted constraint. A model can realize a direction well or misunderstand it. Those differences let the person judge their environment and revise how preference works through it, without requiring a supposedly pure preference outside the environment.

## What selection leaves in history

Selecting one account or design leaves others outside present attention. An omitted impression may never be developed; a rejected passage may disappear from the current text. When the earlier expression or version has been retained, it remains part of the trajectory. A present view and the history of how it formed therefore do different work.

Git/GitHub retains committed versions and their parent relations. Its commit graph is a directed acyclic graph of ancestry, as defined in the [Git glossary](https://git-scm.com/docs/gitglossary#def_DAG). Branches and differences can reveal alternatives and changes that the current file alone conceals. Computer History, within its observation scope, can connect those works with reading, conversation, and use. [Forming a life trajectory](flow-and-time.md) develops their complementary roles.

The graph's edges concern version ancestry, not “preferred to.” A merge records ancestry without proving why a choice was made; an unrecorded possibility cannot be recovered merely from the graph. A person can revisit an older idea while producing a new event in history. These distinctions let history support an account of selection without treating its graph as the preference relation itself.

Retain what makes a consequential choice intelligible: the alternatives actually encountered, what was adopted or set aside, surrounding purposes and conditions, and the response to the result. Existing sources and version history can carry this material; every passing possibility need not become another permanent file.

## Making an implicit selection pattern visible

The model can compare these traces and formulate a pattern the person has not yet articulated. For example, several revisions may replace elaborate navigation with direct passage links. Reading the corresponding requests and use episodes may reveal a preference for preserving connected thought through the environment. The model can show the contrasts and explain the interpretation, giving the person something recognizable to accept, refine, or challenge.

This is more than collecting explicit “I prefer” statements. Selection can already be operative in a repository, prompt, default, or repeated way of working before its content is consciously described. Model interpretation makes that activity inspectable. It remains a revisable account: the same result can have more than one intelligible explanation, and new history can change which relation matters.

Keep the history rich enough to distinguish the alternatives. Choosing direct links in a long argument need not imply disliking every overview. A model's proposal, the person's correction, and the eventual adopted arrangement reveal different contributions to the extended preference. What the model proposes as a pattern should remain connected to those traces, rather than hardening through repeated summaries into an unexplained trait.

## Returning to and revising preference

Making a pattern visible changes what can be judged. A person can ask whether a preference still serves their life, which alternatives it routinely excludes, what costs its realization creates, or whether they want to cultivate another way of choosing. Revising an interface or instruction can then change the preference's practical realization; revising its purpose can change what the environment is designed to support.

This gives the connection with Lacan a specific point. In [“Subversion of the Subject”](https://recherche-lacan.gnipl.fr/2015/07/05/les-ecrits-subversion-du-sujet-et-dialectique-du-desir-dans-linconscient-freudien-1960/), later signifying connections can participate in determining earlier meaning. Here, encountering a comparison of one's earlier choices can disclose a relation that was not previously formulated, or alter how an earlier choice is understood. The original event remains recorded while its place in the current account changes. Applying that question to model-assisted histories is this essay's development; an inferred preference is not automatically a diagnosis or a recovered hidden desire.

The return can also change the premise organizing the alternatives. Someone may initially frame receiving help and retaining direction as mutually exclusive. Their own collaborative work may make another question possible: how should help be organized so they can participate in shaping it? This reconstructs the question rather than simply choosing another answer under the old premise. [Speech, opposition, and self-formation](speech-opposition-and-self-formation.md#conflicting-models-and-reconstructing-the-question) develops that dialectical movement.

Common consequence and reconstructed premises remain different achievements. In the earlier example, both incompatible grounds support $Q(a)$ while the original rules stay fixed. Reconsidering those rules changes the problem. Neither judgment is a mathematical description of a person's development. YinYang's central [crossed participation and return](yinyang-and-taiji.md#returning-through-the-other-the-dialectical-meaning-of-role-change) concerns what the person and counterpart can take up through one another, with both generation and scrutiny retained.

## Preference, psychology, and personal conditions

Preference material asks what is selected, valued, retained, or changed, and how that selection operates through the environment. Psychological material concerns experience, emotion, cognition, relationships, and the evidence available from narratives and assessments. Personal conditions include bodily facts, habits, resources, places, and everyday constraints. These subjects interact while answering different questions.

A person may prefer a mobile working arrangement, experience fatigue while remaining still, and depend on a particular device for some tasks. Keeping those contributions distinct makes their relation easier to understand and revise. An assessment result does not settle what the person values; a preference does not explain its psychological cause; a bodily condition needs its own dated evidence.

Private **Personal** material can organize these subjects, retaining source records and interpretations with their grounds. The six working accounts then draw on them according to their subjects: direction, person, counterpart, relationship, environment, and revision. The person account can bring preferences, psychological material, and life conditions together without reducing the person to any one category. [Forming and revising working models](../design-and-revision.md) develops how this understanding becomes usable collaboration.

## Finish situated tasks amid changing norms and worlds

A finite task can be completed while some alternatives remain unresolved. What matters is whether the selected work answers the actual purpose, and whether an unresolved difference changes that result. History keeps consequential exclusions available for later examination; it need not prevent present action.

## Evolve forward with more capable models

A preference's realization can change with model capability. A stronger counterpart may carry work that formerly required prompts, intermediate procedures, or personal supervision. Keep the purpose intelligible and replace means whose contribution has ended. Judge the new arrangement through actual use, including whether it makes a different way of participating possible. Revisability belongs both to the preference and to the environment through which it is extended.
