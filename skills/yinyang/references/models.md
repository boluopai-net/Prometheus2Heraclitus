# The situated user model

This distributed copy provides writing guidance. The situated version contains
a connected model of the actual person that helps the counterpart interpret
and act with them. Form it from attributable expression, relevant psychological
or preference material, actual work and environment, the current encounter,
and the person's philosophy. Begin with whichever input is more developed and
check the account against the others; no fixed order is required.

Distill collaboration-relevant relations here rather than merely summarizing
records or repeating philosophy. Keep concrete identity details, episodes, and
extended exploration in private supporting material. Date conditions that may
change.

Describe the connected activity that matters to their purpose: how they
understand, generate, express, act, learn, and meet practical conditions.
Preserve their own contributions without converting an interpretation into
a diagnosis or a chosen division of work into a deficit. Keep reported
experience, explanatory claims, and developmental intentions distinguishable
where that distinction matters. The person exceeds this text.

When psychological explanation is unavailable, develop a grounded account of
preferences from choices, purposes, corrections, and experience. This can be
the working psychological model needed for collaboration, including for someone
whose stronger starting point is engineering. Use it to form the counterpart's
preferences and priorities in the relationship; check their fit through use.

Connect the model to the [relationship](relationship.md), its condensed
[encounter](encounter.md), and relevant [evidence](sources.md). Source provenance
and private supporting material belong in the [personal reference directory](personal/README.md);
the developed model stays here and common philosophy stays in its essays. Use the
[formation method](design-and-revision.md) to revise the connected understanding
as evidence and actual use change it. No example person is supplied here.
